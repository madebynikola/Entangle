## Beat-Aligned Lossless ECG Compression Exceeding 3:1 on KL-Patch 72 Data

**Company:** Kardiolink a.d. works in medtech and digital health. [C005] Challenge C005-CH2.  
**Researcher:** Lazar Šarić is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Mathematics and Informatics, Faculty of Sciences. [R0057]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
Kardiolink deploys approximately 2,200 KL-Patch 72 devices per month, each generating 900 MB of 16-bit ECG at 250 Hz across three channels over 72 hours, totalling roughly 2 TB of raw signal per month. [C005-CH2] Current lossless compression achieves only a 3:1 ratio, incurring €7,400 per month in AWS storage costs; at the projected 4,000 devices per month by Q2 next year, this cost becomes unsustainable. [C005-CH2] The regulatory submission is built around lossless compression; switching to lossy methods would require a new clinical validation study and amendments to the technical file. [C005-CH2]

### Research questions
1. Can beat-aligned framing combined with a GRU-based learned entropy model achieve a lossless compression ratio above 3:1 on Kardiolink's 16-bit, 250 Hz, 3-channel wearable ECG data — measured as compression ratio and bit-per-sample on the 140,000-segment labelled dataset, with zero reconstruction error verified on every segment?
2. Does characterising wearable-specific noise (baseline wander and electrode motion artefacts) in a segment-level noise model improve the lossless compression ratio compared with a noise-agnostic baseline — measured as the ratio gain on artefact-labelled segments in the dataset against artefact-free segments?

### Approach
- Profile the 140,000-segment dataset for beat-aligned quasi-periodic structure and wearable noise characteristics (baseline wander, electrode motion artefacts). Use this profiling to set framing parameters and to split the dataset into artefact-heavy and clean subsets for separate benchmarking.
- Apply beat-aligned framing and inter-beat decorrelation, following the segmentation approach from the researcher's prior ECG compression work, to reduce intra-signal redundancy before entropy coding. The framing stage operates at 250 Hz on 16-bit samples and preserves all sample values exactly.
- Train a GRU-based context model to predict the probability distribution of transform-domain ECG coefficients, following the learned entropy model approach from the researcher's prior work, and use it as an arithmetic coder back-end. The coder is constrained to lossless mode: reconstruction error must be zero on all test segments.
- Benchmark the resulting codec against the current 3:1 baseline on the full labelled dataset, reporting compression ratio and decoding latency per segment. Produce a standalone Python reference implementation ready for integration into Kardiolink's cloud ingestion pipeline.

### First six months
- Month 1: NDA is signed; Kardiolink transfers a 5,000-segment representative sample to the researcher's lab for initial profiling; researcher and Kardiolink engineer agree on the zero-error verification protocol.
- Month 2: The master's student runs beat detection and noise labelling on the sample; baseline codecs (FLAC and DCT + arithmetic coder) are benchmarked to confirm the 3:1 starting point and set the improvement target.
- Month 3: Full 140,000-segment dataset is transferred under NDA; the master's student completes dataset profiling and produces inter-beat correlation statistics and a noise-artefact label set.
- Months 4–6: Beat-aligned framing and the GRU-based entropy model are implemented and trained; lossless reconstruction is verified on every segment; Kardiolink engineer reviews integration feasibility.

### Why this pair
Kardiolink's compression bottleneck sits precisely where the researcher's published methods apply: the beat-aligned quasi-periodic structure of ECG is the redundancy that beat-aligned framing and GRU context prediction are designed to exploit, and Kardiolink brings approximately 140,000 labelled segments of real ambulatory wearable ECG data available under NDA, while the researcher brings published methods in beat-aligned framing and GRU-based learned entropy coding for ECG signals and experience leading compression standards work in a telemedicine project with hospital and industrial partners.

- Kardiolink's compression problem is structural: 900 MB of quasi-periodic 16-bit ECG per device per recording, with current lossless codecs reaching only 3:1 and storage costs of €7,400 per month already unsustainable at projected growth. [C005-CH2]
- Šarić co-authored work on beat-aligned framing and segmentation for ECG signals exploiting inter-beat correlations, and co-authored work on learned entropy models for ECG signal compression using GRU-based context prediction for ECG coefficients. [SYN-PUB-00760, SYN-PUB-00757, SYN-PUB-00755, SYN-PUB-00763, SYN-PUB-00761]
- Šarić consulted on ECG compression module selection and parameter tuning for a medical device startup, and led the compression standards work package in a telemedicine project with hospital and industrial partners. [R0057, SYN-PRJ-0214]

### Risks and constraints
- The published work covers ECG compression broadly; evidence of compression specifically tuned to wearable electrode noise and baseline wander profiles of ambulatory patches is not documented in the cited records. [SYN-PUB-00762, SYN-PUB-00760, SYN-PUB-00755]
- The codec evaluation work covers rate-distortion metrics including percentage root-mean-square difference; formal verification against an MDR lossless compliance requirement has not been described in the cited records. [SYN-PUB-00762, SYN-PUB-00760]

### Agreed up front (proposed)
- Publication: results may be published after a 60-day written review by Kardiolink; the company may request redaction of device-specific signal statistics before submission.
- Ownership: Kardiolink owns the trained codec, the Python implementation, and any derivative compression parameters; the University of Novi Sad retains the underlying framing and entropy model methods for further academic use.
- Data access: the 140,000-segment dataset is transferred to the researcher's lab under a signed NDA before work begins; data is stored on university servers with access limited to named project members and deleted or returned at project close.
- Preconditions: NDA must be signed and dataset transfer confirmed before algorithm development starts; processing of any patient-linked data requires ethics approval from the Faculty of Sciences ethics board and compliance with GDPR and the Serbian Law on Personal Data Protection.
