## Signal-Quality Floors for Power-Optimised ECG Acquisition on KL-Patch 72

**Company:** Kardiolink a.d. works in medtech and digital health. [C005] Challenge C005-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Maja Popović is an Associate Professor in the Department of Biomedical Engineering, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0906]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
The KL-Patch 72 runs 250 Hz continuous ECG acquisition and BLE communication on a 54 mAh lithium cell and consumes 18 mAh per day, leaving insufficient margin for active patients who trigger frequent data syncs. [C005-CH1, C005] The challenge requires reducing active current draw by at least 35% without lowering the sampling rate below 250 Hz, the threshold below which ECG clinical validity is affected. [C005-CH1] Firmware changes and BLE tuning have already approached their limits; the company needs power reduction from mechanisms beyond advertising intervals and PHY selection. [C005-CH1] The device must maintain a 72-hour wear period on a single charge. [C005-CH1, C005]

### Research questions
1. What is the minimum sampling rate and ADC resolution at which clinically valid QRS detection, heart rate variability, and arrhythmia classification are preserved on the KL-Patch 72 platform — measured by sensitivity and specificity of these algorithms against the full 250 Hz reference signal on at least 500 hours of recordings from the existing current-profiling dataset?
2. Which combinations of adaptive sampling, MCU sleep-state scheduling, and signal-quality-gated BLE transmission reduce active current draw by 35% or more while keeping signal quality above the validated floor — measured by current draw in mA logged on the Keysight N6705C against the baseline 18 mAh per day figure?

### Approach
- Establish a quantified signal-quality floor using the spectral and morphological assessment methods from Popović's signal quality assessment work, applied to KL-Patch 72 recordings downsampled in software to a range of rates from 125 Hz to 250 Hz, to find where QRS sensitivity, PR interval accuracy, and HRV metrics degrade below clinically acceptable bounds.
- Apply the integer-arithmetic QRS detection and arrhythmia classification algorithms from Popović's ultra-low-power microcontroller work to each candidate sampling configuration, measuring classification accuracy against the 250 Hz reference to determine the tightest configuration that stays above the quality floor.
- Profile the full power budget of each validated configuration on the instrumented reference hardware using the Keysight N6705C, isolating ADC, MCU active and sleep states, and BLE radio contribution per configuration, to identify where the largest savings arise.
- Design a signal-quality-gated transmission scheme: the MCU runs the embedded signal quality index between beats and withholds or batches BLE packets when signal quality is high and the patient is inactive, using activity context from the accelerometer consistent with Popović's accelerometer-based activity recognition work.
- Validate the best configuration end-to-end on the KL-Patch 72 firmware under a 72-hour simulated wear protocol, confirming both the 35% current reduction target and the clinical validity floor simultaneously, and document against the MDR 2017/745 technical file requirements covered in Popović's clinical validation work.

- Month 1: The company provides one instrumented KL-Patch 72 reference unit and grants access to the Keysight N6705C power analyzer for joint measurement sessions.
- Months 1–2: Popović's team builds the software downsampling and signal-quality assessment pipeline using the spectral methods from prior signal quality assessment work; run it across all rate configurations from 125 to 250 Hz and document the degradation curve.
- Month 2: Hold a joint review meeting at which the company's firmware engineers and Popović's team agree on the validated minimum sampling floor and the activity-state definitions that will gate the adaptive sampler.
- Months 3–4: Implement the adaptive sampling scheduler in a firmware branch on the existing FreeRTOS / ARM Cortex-M4 / nRF52840 platform; take the first N6705C current measurements against the baseline 18 mAh per day figure and log the delta.

### Why this pair
The company's hard constraint is clinical: no power-saving measure may cross the 250 Hz signal-quality boundary, but the company has no published methodology to quantify exactly where that boundary lies in practice on its own device, nor which trade-offs between sampling rate, ADC resolution, and BLE scheduling stay safely above it. Popović brings precisely the signal-quality assessment methods, embedded ECG classification algorithms, and MDR-oriented validation experience needed to turn that vague boundary into a measurable floor that the company's firmware engineers can then optimise against with confidence. Neither side could do this alone: the company has the hardware, power tools, and clinical product knowledge but lacks the academic signal-processing and validation methodology; Popović has the methods and clinical framing but no access to the KL-Patch 72 platform, its recordings, or its power instrumentation.

### Agreed up front (proposed)
- Publication: results may be published in a conference paper or journal article after a 60-day review period by Kardiolink a.d. The company may request removal of specific firmware parameters or product identifiers before submission; it may not veto publication on commercial grounds alone.
- Ownership of results: Kardiolink a.d. owns the firmware modifications, the validated minimum-rate configuration, and all measurement logs produced during the project. The Faculty of Technical Sciences retains the signal-quality assessment methodology and benchmark pipeline for continued academic research.
- Data access: KL-Patch 72 recordings transferred to the Faculty lab are used solely for this project, stored on Faculty servers under access controls agreed in writing, and deleted or returned at project close. No recordings leave the Faculty lab without written consent from Kardiolink a.d.
- Precondition — ethics and data protection: because the recordings may contain identifiable cardiac data of patients or study participants, the Faculty must obtain ethics approval from its institutional review board and the data-sharing arrangement must comply with GDPR and the Serbian Law on Personal Data Protection (Official Gazette RS 87/2018) before any data is transferred. This approval is a hard precondition for starting Month 1 work.
- Precondition — IP baseline: both parties document in writing, before project start, which firmware components and algorithms are pre-existing IP of Kardiolink a.d. and which are pre-existing methods of the Faculty, so that ownership of jointly developed artefacts is unambiguous at project close.
