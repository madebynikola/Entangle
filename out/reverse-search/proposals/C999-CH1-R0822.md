## Adaptive Threshold Bearing Fault Detection Under Seasonal Temperature Variation in Wind Turbines

**Company:** Described problem works in renewable energy. [C999] Challenge C999-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Vladana Radić is a Full Professor in the Department of Mechatronics, Robotics and Automation, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0822]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
Vibration sensors on wind turbine gearboxes raise false alarms in cold weather, and bearing faults are still missed in winter despite fixed thresholds per season. [C999-CH1] The company holds two years of labelled vibration data from 40 turbines, and fixed seasonal thresholds fail to prevent missed bearing faults in winter. [C999-CH1]

### Research questions
1. Can a multivariate adaptive threshold model — trained on the labelled two-year dataset and conditioned on ambient temperature as a continuous covariate — reduce false-alarm rate in cold-weather months while keeping missed-fault rate at or below the warm-weather baseline? Measured by precision and recall on held-out winter quarters versus summer quarters of the labelled dataset.
2. Can an adaptive, season-aware fault classifier reduce false alarms in winter while maintaining detection of genuine bearing faults, compared with the current fixed-threshold approach? Measured by false-alarm rate and missed-fault rate on the labelled vibration dataset, split by season.

### Approach
- Extract time-domain and frequency-domain features (RMS, kurtosis, envelope spectrum bands) from the labelled vibration records and build a seasonal baseline per bearing location to distinguish operating-condition variation from fault-driven signal change.
- Apply PCA and Hotelling T² multivariate statistical process control to model the normal operating region as a function of temperature, so that the control boundary shifts continuously with environmental conditions rather than by fixed seasonal step.
- Adapt the patent's adaptive threshold mechanism to use temperature as an additional operating-condition covariate alongside rotational speed, and calibrate threshold trajectories on the warm-weather data before validating on held-out winter data.
- Evaluate the adaptive classifier against the fixed-threshold baseline on the labelled vibration dataset, reporting precision, recall, and false-alarm counts per season.

### First six months
- Month 1: The company transfers the two-year labelled vibration dataset to the university under a signed data-sharing agreement; the master's student sets up the feature extraction and seasonal segmentation pipeline.
- Month 2: The student and the company engineer audit label consistency across all 40 turbines and agree on a train/validation/test split that holds out at least one full winter quarter.
- Month 3: Baseline metrics (false-alarm rate, missed-fault rate by season) are computed on the current fixed-threshold system, establishing the target the adaptive model must beat.
- Months 4–6: Radić's adaptive threshold and PCA-T² methods are implemented and tuned on training data; results on the held-out winter test set are compared against the baseline and reported to the company.

### Why this pair
The company has a clearly bounded signal-processing problem and two years of labelled data that make it solvable within six months — the data asset removes the usual first-year collection risk. Radić brings the three specific technical pieces the problem requires: an adaptive threshold patent that targets variable operating conditions, a multivariate control-chart method that can absorb temperature as a continuous covariate, and sensor-fusion work that jointly models vibration and thermal signals. The company brings two years of labelled turbine vibration data and operational ground truth on fault events; Radić brings adaptive threshold methods, multivariate statistical process control applied to rotating machinery, and sensor fusion of vibration and thermal signals.

### Risks and constraints
- The patent on adaptive threshold setting (SYN-PAT-045) covers variable operating speed; the company's core problem is seasonal temperature variation, and coverage of temperature as a covariate in that patent is not confirmed by the available record. [SYN-PAT-045, C999-CH1]
- The infrared thermography and thermal fusion work (SYN-PUB-08929, SYN-PRJ-0441) covers slow-speed bearings in conveyor and grain handling systems; applicability to wind turbine gearbox speeds would need to be assessed. [SYN-PUB-08929, SYN-PRJ-0441]

- Data access: the company provides the labelled dataset under a signed data-sharing agreement before month 1 begins; the dataset is stored on university infrastructure accessible only to the named project team.
- Preconditions: the project starts upon execution of the data-sharing agreement and confirmation of the company engineer's availability for at least one day per week during the six-month period.
