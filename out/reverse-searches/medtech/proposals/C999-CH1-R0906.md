## Adaptive Accelerometer-Fused ECG Filtering to Restore AF Detection During Walking

**Company:** Described problem works in medical devices. [C999] Challenge C999-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Maja Popović is an Associate Professor in the Department of Biomedical Engineering, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0906]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
The company's wearable ECG patch uses fixed band-pass filtering, and arrhythmia detection sensitivity drops below 80% when the patient walks. [C999-CH1] The company holds 1,200 hours of cardiologist-annotated ECG recordings from 90 patients. [C999-CH1] The company has a wearable ECG patch in use. [C999-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2021 study reports that normalised least mean square adaptive filtering driven by per-electrode inertial measurement units reduces motion contamination from ECG signals in chest-movement scenarios. [W3129854657]

### Research questions
1. Can adaptive filtering fused with simultaneous accelerometer data raise AF detection sensitivity above 80% during walking, measured against cardiologist annotations on the company's 1,200-hour dataset?
2. Does a signal quality index applied before rhythm analysis further increase AF detection specificity during movement, measured as reduction in false-positive AF episodes on the same annotated dataset?

### Approach
- Apply the adaptive filtering method fused with accelerometer data from the researcher's prior work to the company's ECG recordings, tuning the filter against the fixed band-pass baseline on cardiologist-annotated segments.
- Use the signal quality assessment method from the researcher's prior work to flag low-quality segments before RR-interval analysis, preventing corrupt beats from entering the AF detector.
- Apply the RR-interval irregularity and rhythm analysis method from the researcher's prior work to the cleaned signal, measuring sensitivity and specificity against cardiologist AF annotations on walking segments.
- Assess whether the adaptive filtering and QRS detection steps can be expressed in integer arithmetic suitable for the patch's microcontroller, using the ultra-low-power algorithm design principles from the researcher's prior work.

### Plan

| Period | Work | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Months 1–3 | A master's student in the researcher's group, with the company's engineer for one day per week, audits the annotated dataset, labels walking versus rest segments, and measures the baseline sensitivity of the current fixed band-pass filter plus existing AF detector on those segments. | A characterised baseline: sensitivity by activity level, a labelled subset of walking segments, and a shared data pipeline. |
| Months 4–6 | The master's student implements the adaptive accelerometer-fused filter and signal quality index, tunes them on a training split of the walking segments, and evaluates on the held-out split. The company's engineer validates that AF annotations are applied consistently. | A validated algorithm showing whether sensitivity exceeds 80% on walking segments; an interim technical report. |
| Months 7–9 | The team assesses integer-arithmetic feasibility on the patch's microcontroller and produces a final algorithm specification. The researcher and company engineer review findings for a joint publication. | A final report with the algorithm specification, an embedded feasibility assessment, and a draft paper submitted for publication. |

### First six months
- Week 1–2: The company transfers a de-identified subset of the annotated ECG recordings to the researcher's group under a signed data-sharing agreement; the researcher's group sets up a processing environment.
- Month 1: The master's student and the company's engineer jointly define the labelling protocol for walking versus rest segments and apply it to the full dataset.
- Month 2: The master's student runs the current fixed band-pass filter pipeline on walking segments and records the baseline sensitivity figure, producing a reference benchmark both sides agree on.
- Month 3: The researcher and the company's engineer review the benchmark, confirm the target (sensitivity above 80% on walking segments), and finalise the algorithm design plan for months 4–6.

### Why this pair
The company brings a well-defined signal-processing failure on cardiologist-annotated real-world data; the researcher brings adaptive filtering and AF detection methods validated on wearable ECG. Maja Popović brings the specific combination the problem requires — adaptive accelerometer-fused filtering and AF detection validated on walking subjects — plus prior work on embedded algorithm design that the deployment constraint demands. Neither side could do this alone: the company provides the annotated patient data and the deployed hardware target that give the research its clinical validity; the researcher brings the adaptive filtering methods and AF detection pipeline that the company does not have.

[SYN-PUB-09638, SYN-PUB-09643, C999-CH1]

### Agreed up front (proposed)
- Publication: results may be submitted for publication after a 30-day review period by the company; the company may request a further 30-day delay to file a patent if patentable results arise.
- Ownership: the company owns the trained models, filter configurations, and any algorithm specification produced; the University of Novi Sad retains the underlying methods and may use them in further research.
- Data access: the company transfers de-identified ECG recordings to the researcher's group under a signed data-sharing agreement compliant with GDPR and the Serbian Personal Data Protection Act; data is used only for this project and deleted at its end.
- Preconditions: the project starts only after the data-sharing agreement is signed and ethics approval is obtained for secondary use of patient recordings.
