## Volt-Var Smart Inverter Control to Eliminate Overvoltage Disconnections at 300 Solar Sites

**Company:** Described problem works in solar energy. [C999] Challenge C999-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Aleksandar Jeremić is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Power, Electronics and Telecommunications, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0696]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
Rooftop solar inverters at 300 sites disconnect from the grid on hot summer afternoons when local grid voltage rises too high, and the company has tried lowering the export limit to address this. [C999-CH1] The company's current workaround — reducing the export limit — recovers grid connectivity but costs approximately 8% of yearly energy yield across the portfolio. [C999-CH1] The company holds five years of inverter logs and grid voltage readings from 300 sites. [C999-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2021 study compared four reactive power control techniques for solar PV inverters in a low-voltage network and found that incorporating reactive power controls of solar PV inverters aids in successfully mitigating overvoltage issues. [W3177274304]

### Research questions
1. Can a Volt-Var or droop-based reactive power control strategy, parameterised from the five-year inverter and voltage log dataset, prevent inverter disconnections at peak-generation hours — measured as the number of disconnection events in a held-out summer season reduced to zero, with no reduction in active power export relative to the uncurtailed baseline?
2. Which feeder segments and site clusters drive the majority of overvoltage events — measured by hosting capacity analysis against the 300-site voltage logs, producing a ranked map of congestion severity?
3. Does a two-stage control scheme (day-ahead Volt-Var scheduling plus real-time droop correction) outperform a fixed power-factor strategy on the same feeders — measured by peak voltage exceedance margin and reactive power losses across the simulated summer period?

### Approach
- Parse and quality-screen five years of inverter logs and grid voltage readings from 300 sites; reconstruct feeder topology and load/generation time series to build a validated steady-state power-flow model using backward–forward sweep analysis as in the researcher's feeder studies.
- Apply probabilistic hosting capacity assessment — using stochastic simulation and feeder classification — to rank the 300 sites by overvoltage contribution and identify the feeders where reactive power headroom is sufficient to substitute for active power curtailment.
- Design Volt-Var control curves and a two-stage reactive power coordination scheme (day-ahead scheduling with real-time droop correction) calibrated to the identified feeders; validate the design against the five-year dataset using time-series power-flow simulation, targeting zero disconnection events with no export reduction.
- Test the preferred control parameterisation in a hardware-in-the-loop environment using the researcher's real-time digital simulator to confirm dynamic stability before the company configures inverters in the field.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: the company transfers five years of inverter logs and voltage readings (anonymised per data-sharing agreement) to a secure repository accessible to the researcher's group; the company's engineer provides feeder topology diagrams for the 20 sites with the most recorded disconnections.
- Month 1: the master's student sets up the power-flow modelling environment and runs a data-quality audit; disconnection events are extracted and linked to voltage exceedance timestamps across all 300 sites.
- Months 2–3: feeder models for the ten highest-severity sites are built and validated against historical voltage readings; hosting capacity is assessed for these sites and results reviewed jointly by Jeremić and the company's engineer.
- Months 4–6: stochastic hosting capacity assessment scales to all 300 sites; Volt-Var control curves are designed and first time-series simulations run; interim results presented to the company with a go/no-go recommendation on the control approach before hardware-in-the-loop work begins.

### Why this pair
The company brings five years of multi-site operational data from 300 sites; Jeremić brings the hosting capacity methodology and hardware-in-the-loop testing capability needed to turn that data into a validated control design. Jeremić brings the published Volt-Var and reactive power coordination methods and the real-time digital simulator needed to validate the design before field deployment; the company brings the operational dataset from 300 sites and the inverter fleet on which the validated design would run. - Data access: the company provides the five-year log dataset under a data-sharing agreement signed before work begins; the dataset is stored on infrastructure accessible only to named project members and is deleted or returned at project end.
- Preconditions: the data-sharing agreement and any required consent from the regional distribution system operator must be in place before the master's student begins data processing in Month 1.
