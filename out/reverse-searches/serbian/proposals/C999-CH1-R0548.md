## Domain-Adaptive Deep Classifier to Reduce Misgraded Apple Returns on a Sorting Line

**Company:** Described problem works in prerada hrane. [C999] Challenge C999-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Nikola Stojanović is an Associate Professor in the Department of Computing and Control Engineering, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0548]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
The company's sorting line uses a fixed-threshold camera system that reaches only ~88% accuracy on apple grading, causing customer returns of misclassified fruit. [C999-CH1] The two defect types driving misclassification are minor bark damage and early rot, which the current rule-based thresholds cannot reliably separate. [C999-CH1] The company holds 40,000 labeled apple images collected across three harvest seasons, but the fixed-threshold system does not exploit them. [C999-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2022 study reports 92.42% average accuracy on apple defect classification using semantic segmentation combined with a pruned YOLO V4 detector on a conveyor system. [W4304128474]
- A 2022 study reports 94.8% accuracy in classifying apple decay and bruise under uneven illumination using texture features and an SVM model. [W4206192560]

### Research questions
1. Can a deep classifier trained with domain randomisation on the three-season image archive exceed 88% accuracy on bark-damage and early-rot cases, measured by precision, recall and F1 against the company's labelled hold-out set from the most recent season?
2. Can active learning, applied to the existing 40,000 labelled images, identify and prioritise the hardest bark-damage and early-rot cases so that a targeted re-annotation round of under 2,000 images yields a measurable accuracy gain over the baseline classifier?

### Approach
- Train a baseline deep classifier on the full 40,000-image archive, stratified by harvest season, to establish the current accuracy ceiling and identify which defect classes and seasonal splits drive the most errors.
- Apply domain randomisation techniques—colour-jitter, lighting simulation and texture augmentation—to the training data to reduce the seasonal appearance shift that causes the fixed-threshold system to fail across harvests.
- Use the active learning framework based on ensemble uncertainty and diversity sampling to score the existing labelled set, surface the hardest bark-damage and early-rot images, and run a targeted re-annotation pass of the highest-uncertainty cases.
- Evaluate the final classifier on the sorting-line camera stream, measuring accuracy, precision and recall per defect class against the labelled hold-out set, with the target of exceeding the current 88% accuracy baseline.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: The company transfers the 40,000 labeled images (with season and defect-class metadata) to the researcher's group under a signed data-sharing agreement; the master's student sets up the training environment.
- Month 1: The master's student audits label quality, splits the archive into season-stratified train/validation/test sets, and confirms the class distribution of bark-damage and early-rot images.
- Month 2–3: Baseline CNN classifier trained and evaluated; per-class and per-season error rates documented and shared with the company engineer for review.
- Month 4–5: Domain randomisation augmentations applied; active learning pipeline run on the full labelled archive; company engineer reviews the flagged hard cases and confirms re-annotation priority list.

### Why this pair
The company has a well-defined accuracy gap (88%), three seasons of labelled data, and a sorting line with a camera system—the conditions needed to train and test a classifier. Stojanović brings domain randomisation for cross-domain transfer in visual defect detection and active learning to prioritise annotation effort on the hard bark-damage and early-rot cases in the existing archive. Neither side could do it alone: the company provides the only asset that makes the result credible on a real sorting line (production images and live camera hardware), while the researcher provides the methods that turn that archive into a classifier that generalises.

- The sorting line's fixed-threshold system reaches only ~88% accuracy, and Stojanović co-authored domain randomisation for cross-domain visual transfer in defect detection. [C999-CH1, SYN-PUB-06393]
- Bark-damage and early-rot are the defect types driving misclassification on the sorting line, and Stojanović co-authored an active learning framework that prioritises uncertain cases to optimise annotation effort. [C999-CH1, SYN-PUB-06396]
- The VISIONQC project develops a modular inspection platform for SMEs with minimal labelled data requirements and includes pilot deployments at regional manufacturing SMEs. [SYN-PRJ-0011]

### Risks and constraints
- Stojanović's cited defect detection publications cover reflective metal parts, polymer assembly-line components, and manufacturing processes; none covers organic produce or food-surface texture. [SYN-PUB-06388, SYN-PUB-06391, SYN-PUB-06393]
- The active learning framework (SYN-PUB-06396) is validated with industrial partners producing metal stampings and injection-moulded plastic components; the record does not cover multi-season labeled datasets of organic produce. [SYN-PUB-06396]

### Agreed up front (proposed)
- Publication: results may be published after a 30-day review by the company; the company may request removal of commercially sensitive sorting-line parameters before submission.
- Ownership: the company owns the trained model weights and any integrated software; the researcher's group retains the domain randomisation and active learning code as general-purpose research tools.
- Data access: the company provides the 40,000-image archive to the researcher's group for the duration of the project under a data-sharing agreement; images are stored on the researcher's institutional servers and deleted or returned at project close.
- Preconditions: the data-sharing agreement is signed and the image archive is transferred before the master's student begins Month 1 work; the company names one engineer as the day-to-day contact for re-annotation and camera integration.
