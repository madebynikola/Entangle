## Unsupervised Autoencoder Baseline Learning for On-Device ICS Protocol Anomaly Detection

**Company:** ArmaturaSec d.o.o. works in cybersecurity. [C001] Challenge C001-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Vuk Đukić is an Associate Professor in the Department of Computing and Control Engineering, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R1015]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
ArmaturaSec has collected several terabytes of Modbus and BACnet traffic captures from consenting client sites over three years, but no confirmed malicious traffic examples exist. [C001-CH1] Supervised classifiers trained on public ICS datasets produced unacceptable false positive rates in real deployments and were switched off by clients. [C001-CH1, C001/blog/2024-05-20/Why We Stopped Trying to Use Public ICS Datasets for Anomaly Detection] ArmaturaSec intends to release a hardware variant with a dedicated security processor to support on-device anomaly detection without affecting packet forwarding latency. [C001] Anomaly alerts must be explainable in plain language to plant managers. [C001-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2024 study reports that a composite autoencoder model detects anomalous behaviour in ICS network traffic without pre-labelled data, using the HAI benchmark dataset. [W4391996970]
- A 2023 study reports an isolation-forest anomaly detection model that trains on an ESP32 microcontroller in 1.2 to 6.4 seconds and detects anomalies in under 16 milliseconds using 80 KB of RAM. [W4321372735]

### Research questions
1. Can an autoencoder trained solely on normal Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP traffic from a single client site detect behavioural deviations with a false positive rate low enough that plant managers do not disable alerts? Measured as alerts-per-clean-week on three held-out site captures from the company's archives.
2. Can the trained model be compressed and deployed on the ProtoSense hardware without adding measurable latency to packet forwarding? Measured as per-packet inference time and forwarding delay before and after model deployment on the target device.
3. Do reconstruction-error explanations expressed as protocol-field deltas communicate the anomaly cause clearly enough for plant managers to decide on a response? Measured by structured feedback from at least five plant-manager interviews conducted during the field trial.

### Approach
- Extract per-session and per-message features from the company's Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP captures, then train a site-specific autoencoder — using the autoencoder architecture from the researcher's published ICS anomaly detection work — on normal traffic only, with no labelled attack examples.
- Apply the model compression and quantisation techniques from the researcher's work on intrusion detection for resource-constrained IoT devices to reduce model footprint and inference time, targeting deployment on the ProtoSense embedded Linux environment without touching the packet-forwarding path.
- Generate reconstruction-error explanations at the protocol-field level (function code, register address, value range) so that each alert names the specific field that deviated; validate that plant managers can interpret these explanations through structured interviews during the field trial.
- Run a sliding-window recalibration procedure on held-out normal traffic to set alert thresholds per site, avoiding any reliance on public ICS attack datasets.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: Company and researcher sign NDA and data-transfer agreement; company engineer identifies which site captures are cleared for transfer and prepares anonymised copies.
- Week 3–6: Master's student from the researcher's group sets up a parsing pipeline for Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP pcap files, cross-checked against the company's firmware protocol parser, and produces a first feature matrix.
- Month 2–3: Vuk Đukić and the master's student train a first autoencoder on one site's normal traffic, measure reconstruction-error distributions, and report a baseline false positive rate to the company engineer.
- Month 4–6: Apply compression and quantisation to the trained model; benchmark inference time on a ProtoSense hardware unit supplied by the company; deliver a go/no-go report on on-device feasibility before the field trial.
