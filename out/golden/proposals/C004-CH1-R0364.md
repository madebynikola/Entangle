## Kriging-with-External-Drift Parcel Rainfall Estimation to Calibrate Parametric Insurance Triggers

**Company:** OsiguroMi d.o.o. works in insurtech — embedded micro-insurance and parametric products. [C004] Challenge C004-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Žarko Pejić is a Research Associate in the Remote Sensing and GIS Group, BioSense Institute. [R0364]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
OsiguroMi's parametric payout trigger is based on the nearest official weather station, which can be up to 30 km from a farmer's parcel, and terrain variation means a drought signal at the station can coincide with local rain on the field, or vice versa. [C004-CH1] The company holds access to RHMZS data and three private agro-meteorological networks totalling about 140 measurement points, but these have not yet been fused into a spatially continuous parcel-level rainfall product. [C004-CH1, C004] The payout trigger is a fixed contractual threshold that cannot be replaced; the correction must come from more accurate spatial rainfall estimation at the parcel level. [C004-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2022 study reports that optimized kriging of relative rainfall anomalies against long-term means produces 30-year, 250-metre monthly grids from 622 stations, with leave-one-out cross-validation R² of 0.78 over complex terrain. [W4213059839]
- A 2021 study reports that selecting and rank-transforming personal weather station observations before kriging improves spatial interpolation of high-intensity precipitation events at 1-, 3-, 6-, 12-, and 24-hour aggregations. [W3005806384]

### Research questions
1. Can kriging with external drift, blending OsiguroMi's ~140-station network with IMERG satellite estimates, produce parcel-scale daily rainfall grids with lower leave-one-out cross-validation error than the single nearest-station baseline? Measured by RMSE and bias against withheld stations across the covered region.
2. How much does the basis risk — the probability that a parcel experiences drought while the trigger station does not, or vice versa — decrease when the corrected spatial grid replaces the raw station reading as input to the fixed contractual threshold? Measured by burn analysis over the available historical record across a sample of cadastral parcels.
3. Does the downscaling model transfer to the expanded network geometry expected in Bulgaria and Romania, where the company plans to expand its agro line through regional cooperatives? Measured by cross-validation RMSE on a held-out subset of stations simulating lower network density.

### Approach
- Apply kriging with external drift using IMERG satellite precipitation as the external covariate and OsiguroMi's ~140-station gauge network as the primary observations, producing daily parcel-scale rainfall grids for the covered region — following the field-scale downscaling method Pejić co-authored.
- Perform bias correction of the satellite covariate against the gauge network using multi-source blending (ERA5, IMERG) as documented in Pejić's prior work, to remove systematic over- or under-estimation before the kriging step.
- Run burn analysis on the resulting historical parcel-level rainfall series, using the fixed contractual threshold unchanged, to quantify how basis risk changes relative to the nearest-station baseline — applying the insurance calibration method from Pejić's crop index insurance publications.
- Validate the spatial model through leave-one-out cross-validation on the gauge network and test transferability to lower-density configurations representative of the Bulgaria and Romania expansion.

### First six months
- Weeks 1–3: OsiguroMi's internal researcher exports historical station time series from PostgreSQL and shares them with Pejić under the signed data agreement; Pejić downloads the matching IMERG archive for the region.
- Weeks 4–6: Pejić's group runs quality control and bias correction on all four station sources and the satellite covariate; OsiguroMi's engineers flag any gaps or format inconsistencies.
- Months 2–3: Pejić fits the kriging-with-external-drift model on the merged dataset and delivers leave-one-out cross-validation results;
- Months 4–5: OsiguroMi's internal researcher connects the daily grid to the Python loss model; burn analysis runs with the fixed contractual threshold; basis-risk reduction is measured against the nearest-station baseline.

### What each side brings
- **Company:** Access to RHMZS data and three private agro-meteorological networks totalling about 140 measurement points across the region. [C004-CH1, C004] A Python environment for actuarial modelling and trigger calibration, and PostgreSQL databases with time series for historical losses. [C004] One internal researcher for data modelling and two senior engineers who can dedicate part of their capacity to joint projects. [C004]
- **Faculty:** Žarko Pejić co-authored a method for kriging with external drift that blends IMERG satellite estimates with gauge networks to produce field-scale rainfall grids. [SYN-PUB-04461] Pejić co-authored burn analysis and basis risk assessment for crop index insurance across cadastral municipalities and sample parcels. [SYN-PUB-04462, SYN-PUB-04014] Pejić co-authored ordinary kriging methods for monthly rainfall mapping and a Bayesian kriging approach for merging gauge and satellite precipitation data. [SYN-PUB-04460, SYN-PUB-04463] Pejić provided technical consultancy to an agricultural insurance intermediary in 2023 on spatial resolution requirements for parametric rainfall products. [R0364]

### Why this pair
OsiguroMi has the station network, the historical loss data, and a working insurance pipeline, but needs the spatial estimation method to correct basis risk without touching the contractual threshold. Pejić brings the specific combination of kriging-with-external-drift downscaling and crop index insurance calibration that the problem requires; the company brings the proprietary multi-network gauge data and the operational context that turns the model into a live product. Neither could do it alone: without the company's approximately 140-station gauge network the spatial interpolation model has no operational grounding in the region, and without the researcher's published downscaling and burn-analysis methods the company has no validated path from raw station data to corrected parcel-level triggers.

- OsiguroMi's payout trigger is based on official stations up to 30 km from a farmer's parcel, and the goal is to correct payout logic through more accurate spatial estimation without replacing the contractual threshold. [C004-CH1]
- Pejić co-authored a kriging-with-external-drift method blending IMERG satellite estimates with gauge networks to produce field-scale rainfall grids. [SYN-PUB-04461]

### Risks and constraints
- The publications on multi-source bias correction cover ERA5, IMERG, and TMPA; coverage of the specific private agro-meteorological networks OsiguroMi holds is not documented in the record. [SYN-PUB-04014, SYN-PUB-04464, SYN-PUB-04463]
- The company requires results applicable within six to nine months. [C004]

### Agreed up front (proposed)
- Publication: results may be published after a 30-day review by OsiguroMi; the company may request redaction of commercially sensitive network configurations or loss ratios before submission.
- Ownership: OsiguroMi owns the fitted model, trained parameters, and any operational grid product; BioSense Institute retains the underlying geostatistical methods and may reuse them in future research.
- Data access: OsiguroMi provides station time series under a signed data-sharing agreement before work begins; Pejić's group uses the data solely for this project and deletes copies on project close.
- Precondition: the data-sharing agreement covering all four station sources (RHMZS and three private networks) must be signed before the dataset audit in Month 1 can start.
