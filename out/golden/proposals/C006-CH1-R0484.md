## Synthetic Augmentation and Transfer Learning for Serbian Commercial-Dispute Outcome Prediction

**Company:** PravniPilot a.d. works in legal tech. [C006] Challenge C006-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Jovan Puzović is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computing and Control Engineering, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0484]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
A case-outcome prediction model trained on roughly 9,000 labelled Serbian commercial-dispute decisions achieves 61% accuracy, and the company estimates 40,000 clean labelled decisions are needed to reach accuracy worth advertising. [C006-CH1] Basic data augmentation and fine-tuning of pre-trained multilingual models have not moved accuracy past 63% in internal tests. [C006-CH1] Serbian courts do not publish decisions in a consistent machine-readable format, requiring significant cleaning before any decision can be used for training. [C006-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2023 study reports that multi-language transfer learning with extractive and abstractive techniques outperforms state-of-the-art extractive summarization on a low-resource legal corpus, setting a new baseline for abstractive summarization. [W4387008943]
- A 2023 study reports that a translate-and-test pipeline separating translation from classification significantly improves cross-lingual text classification over competitive multilingual baselines on three benchmark datasets. [W4386902339]

### Research questions
1. Can conditional language-model augmentation and rule-based paraphrasing, applied to the company's ~9,000 labelled decisions, raise outcome-prediction accuracy to 75% on a held-out test set — measured against two independent annotators' consensus labels on at least 1,000 withheld decisions?
2. Does intermediate pretraining on Croatian and Bosnian commercial-dispute decisions before fine-tuning on Serbian decisions improve accuracy beyond what Serbian-only training achieves — measured by macro-F1 on the same held-out set?
3. Does a retrieval-augmented prediction layer, retrieving the five most similar decisions from the full corpus at inference time, further improve accuracy over the augmentation-and-transfer baseline — measured on the same held-out test set?

### Approach
- Audit and stratify the existing ~9,000 labelled decisions by outcome class and court level; establish a fixed train/validation/test split so all subsequent experiments are comparable.
- Apply conditional language-model augmentation and rule-based paraphrasing to the training partition, targeting the under-represented outcome classes, then evaluate each augmentation variant against the validation set using evaluation protocols that incorporate domain expert judgment.
- Run intermediate pretraining on Croatian and Bosnian commercial-dispute decisions drawn from the company's indexed corpus, then fine-tune the resulting model on Serbian decisions; compare macro-F1 against the augmentation-only baseline.
- Add a retrieval-augmented prediction layer that retrieves similar decisions at inference time using dense retrieval; combine retrieved context with the fine-tuned model and evaluate on the held-out test set against the 75% accuracy target; package the final pipeline for direct integration into the production codebase.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: The company exports the ~9,000 labelled decisions to a secure shared environment; the researcher and data engineer agree on the data-sharing protocol and split strategy.
- Week 3–6: The master's student sets up the training and evaluation infrastructure; the current 61% baseline is reproduced and logged so all future experiments are comparable.
- Month 2–3: The master's student and data engineer audit class balance, label quality, and format inconsistencies; the researcher reviews and proposes the augmentation plan.
- Month 4–6: Conditional language-model augmentation and rule-based paraphrasing are implemented on the training partition; a sample of synthetic decisions is validated by the company's annotators using the internal annotation tool.

### Why this pair
Standard multilingual fine-tuning has not moved accuracy past 63%; the remaining gap calls for synthetic augmentation — methods Puzović has published on for legal NLP — and cross-jurisdictional transfer learning, for which Puzović has published work specifically on Croatian–Serbian legal text. Neither could do this alone: the company owns the labelled corpus, the annotation tool, and the multilingual index of two million decisions that makes transfer learning possible, while the researcher brings the augmentation and retrieval-augmented prediction methods that go beyond what the company's internal team has tried.

- PravniPilot's model achieves 61% accuracy; internal fine-tuning experiments have not moved accuracy past 63%, and the company targets 75% accuracy on a held-out test set within 12 months. [C006-CH1]
- Puzović co-authored work on synthetic data generation for legal NLP and on transfer learning with intermediate pretraining on a Croatian–Serbian multilingual legal corpus. [SYN-PUB-05710, SYN-PUB-05711, SYN-PUB-05708]
- The company has processed over two million court decisions and indexes them using PostgreSQL and Elasticsearch. [C006]

### Risks and constraints
- Puzović co-authored a retrieval-augmented generation study on Serbian civil court decisions; the company's target domain is commercial-dispute decisions. [SYN-PUB-02145, C006-CH1]

## Where to apply

No open EU call fits this work. The pair's problem is outcome prediction on Serbian court decisions using synthetic data generation, transfer learning across Balkan jurisdictions, and retrieval-augmented methods to reach 75% accuracy. The calls cover AI in healthcare (Virtual Human Twins, health adaptation to climate change), media and journalism (EU AI Media Platform, audiovisual production), cybersecurity, disaster management, quantum computing, forestry, and general digital skills. None of these domains match legal tech, judicial decision support, or multilingual NLP for low-resource court data. Even the cybersecurity AI call targets threats and vulnerabilities, not outcome prediction.
