## Synthetic Augmentation and Cross-Lingual Transfer for Serbian Court-Decision Outcome Prediction

**Company:** PravniPilot a.d. works in legal tech. [C006] Challenge C006-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Jovan Puzović is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computing and Control Engineering, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0484]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
A case-outcome prediction model trained on roughly 9,000 labeled Serbian commercial-dispute decisions achieves 61% accuracy and has plateaued near 63% despite basic data augmentation and fine-tuned multilingual pre-trained models. [C006-CH1] The company estimates it needs at least 40,000 clean labeled decisions to reach the target 75% accuracy, but Serbian courts do not publish decisions in a consistent machine-readable format, making it hard to scale the labeled corpus without substantial manual effort. [C006-CH1] PravniPilot must reach 75% accuracy on a held-out test set within 12 months to market the prediction feature with honest confidence intervals. [C006-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2025 study on low-resource Sindhi text classification reports that EDA, particularly Random Deletion, achieves 99% F1 for binary classification on 3,364 news articles using a fine-tuned multilingual BERT model, while back-translation and paraphrasing also deliver substantial improvements. [W4411347520]
- A 2021 systematic comparison reports that text augmentation at the token and syntax levels improves sequence tagging for low-resource languages when combined with pretrained multilingual contextualized models such as mBERT, with gains most pronounced on morphologically rich languages. [W3214193653]

### Research questions
1. Can synthetic legal text generation — using conditional language models and back-translation from Croatian and Bosnian decisions — combined with cost-sensitive fine-tuning lift case-outcome prediction accuracy from 63% to at least 75% on the held-out Serbian commercial-dispute test set, measured by macro-averaged accuracy and F1 on that fixed set?
2. Which combination of cross-lingual transfer source (Croatian only, Bosnian only, both) and intermediate pretraining strategy produces the largest accuracy gain on the Serbian test set, measured by ablation on the same held-out set?
3. Does a semi-supervised labeling step applied to unlabeled decisions in the two-million-decision corpus increase accuracy further, measured by comparing the fully supervised and semi-supervised models on the held-out test set?

### Approach
- Extract and align Croatian and Bosnian decisions from the existing corpus using the company's PDF/HTML parser and annotation tool; apply Puzović's intermediate pretraining strategy — domain-adaptive pretraining on unlabeled Serbian, Croatian and Bosnian legal text — to adapt a multilingual transformer before fine-tuning on the 9,000 labeled Serbian decisions.
- Apply Puzović's synthetic legal text generation pipeline — conditional language-model generation and back-translation — to the labeled Serbian corpus to produce additional training examples; validate synthetic-data quality against the company's annotators using the domain-expertise evaluation protocol from prior work.
- Fine-tune the augmented model with cost-sensitive learning objectives (focal loss, weighted cross-entropy) from Puzović's imbalanced-classification work to address any class skew in case outcomes, targeting the 75% accuracy threshold on the held-out test set.
- Package the trained model and inference pipeline as a Python/PyTorch module with a PostgreSQL-compatible serving wrapper, integrable directly into the production codebase, and run a final ablation to report each component's contribution.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: The company's data engineer shares a sample of 500 Serbian, Croatian and Bosnian decisions with the researcher's group under a signed data-access agreement; the two sides agree on the fixed held-out test-set composition and label schema.
- Month 1: The master's student installs the pretraining pipeline on the company's PyTorch stack using the company's corpus index; the data engineer validates the parser output for Croatian and Bosnian formats.
- Month 2: Intermediate pretraining runs on the unlabeled multi-jurisdiction text; the master's student reports a baseline fine-tuned accuracy on the held-out test set to confirm the 63% starting point.
- Month 3: Synthetic text generation and back-translation pipeline is set up; the company's annotators evaluate the first batch of synthetic examples for legal plausibility; go/no-go decision on each augmentation strategy before month 4.

### Why this pair
The company has a large, well-indexed corpus and the annotation infrastructure to scale labeling, but its in-house team has exhausted standard fine-tuning approaches and cannot independently design synthetic generation or cross-lingual pretraining pipelines for a low-resource South Slavic legal setting. Puzović brings exactly the methods the problem needs — synthetic legal text generation, cost-sensitive imbalanced classification, and domain-adaptive pretraining on small legal corpora — but would have no access to a two-million-decision proprietary corpus or to domain annotators able to validate synthetic legal output. Together they can close the data gap that neither side can close alone.
