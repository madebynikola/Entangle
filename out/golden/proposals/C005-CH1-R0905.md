## Subsystem Power-Budget Audit and Redesign to Cut KL-Patch 72 Active Current by 35%

**Company:** Kardiolink a.d. works in medtech and digital health. [C005] Challenge C005-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Filip Mijatović is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Power, Electronics and Telecommunications, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0905]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
The KL-Patch 72 consumes 18 mAh per day when Bluetooth is active and runs on a 54 mAh lithium cell, so active patients who trigger frequent syncs risk battery depletion before the 72-hour wear period ends. [C005-CH1, C005] The company needs to reduce active current draw by at least 35% while maintaining 250 Hz ECG sampling; clinical signal validity depends on holding that sampling floor. [C005-CH1] Kardiolink has already tuned BLE advertising intervals, moved to BLE 5.0 long-range PHY, and switched to a lower-power MCU variant, and is now within a few milliamps of what firmware changes alone can achieve. [C005-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2021 study reports multi-level power optimization of an ambulatory EEG monitoring system on a reconfigurable processor, achieving 73.1% energy reduction over software-only implementation while maintaining real-time seizure detection. [W3206057408]
- A 2022 study reports experimental characterisation of packet forwarding efficiency and duty-cycle configurations for battery-powered BLE relay nodes across multiple scenarios. [W3217361478]

### Research questions
1. Can a redesigned analog front-end and ADC operating point, derived from sub-threshold biopotential circuit techniques, reduce the combined AFE-plus-ADC active current by at least 35% on the KL-Patch 72 reference hardware at 250 Hz, measured with the Keysight N6705C against the current baseline profile?
2. Can event-driven BLE radio scheduling, adapted from wake-up-receiver and duty-cycle MAC principles, cut radio-subsystem current during active wear without dropping a sync cycle, measured as total radio energy per 72-hour session on the reference hardware?
3. Does the combined AFE, ADC, and radio optimisation meet the ≥35% whole-device active-current reduction target while ECG signal quality, assessed against a clinical reference recording at 250 Hz, remains unaffected?

### Approach
- Conduct a full active-current audit using the Keysight N6705C: record per-subsystem current waveforms for the AFE, ADC, BLE radio, and MCU under a standardised 72-hour wear simulation to establish a numbered baseline and identify the dominant consumers.
- Apply sub-threshold and low-quiescent-current biopotential front-end design principles — drawn from published ultra-low-power mixed-signal AFE work — to reconfigure or redesign the analog front-end and ADC operating points on the nRF52840 platform, targeting reduced quiescent current without altering the 250 Hz sampling path.
- Adapt event-driven idle-state management and duty-cycle slot-allocation principles — drawn from wake-up-receiver and MAC scheduling research — to the BLE stack on FreeRTOS, scheduling radio activity around ECG data bursts to reduce radio-on time without missing sync deadlines.
- Validate the combined design on the instrumented reference hardware: measure whole-device active current and record a 250 Hz ECG trace for clinical signal-quality comparison, confirming the ≥35% reduction target is met.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: Kardiolink provides the master's student with access to the instrumented reference hardware and the Keysight N6705C; both teams agree on the wear-simulation protocol and the current-measurement procedure.
- Month 1: The master's student runs the baseline audit, records per-subsystem current waveforms, and produces the numbered current profile. Filip Mijatović reviews the profile and sets the optimisation priorities.
- Months 2–3: Filip Mijatović identifies AFE and ADC reconfiguration options from sub-threshold front-end design principles; the master's student implements and measures each change on the reference hardware, confirming the 250 Hz sampling floor is held at each step.
- Months 4–6: The master's student and the company firmware engineer jointly adapt duty-cycle radio scheduling for the BLE stack under FreeRTOS; Filip Mijatović reviews the MAC-layer changes against deadline-aware scheduling principles; incremental measurements track progress toward the 35% target.

### Why this pair
The KL-Patch 72 problem is a subsystem-level power engineering challenge that standard firmware tuning cannot solve; it requires expertise in ultra-low-power analog front-end design and radio duty-cycle scheduling applied to specific measurement hardware. Mijatović brings published methods in sub-microwatt biopotential AFE design and event-driven MAC scheduling that map directly onto the AFE, ADC, and BLE radio subsystems identified in the audit. Neither side could complete this alone: Kardiolink has the instrumented hardware, the BLE platform, and the clinical constraints, while Mijatović brings the circuit and protocol-layer techniques needed to find the remaining headroom those constraints permit.

- The KL-Patch 72 consumes 18 mAh per day on a 54 mAh cell and must cut active current by at least 35% at a fixed 250 Hz floor, after all firmware optimisations are exhausted. [C005-CH1, C005]
- Filip Mijatović co-authored work on analog front-end circuits for biopotential acquisition achieving sub-microwatt quiescent currents and on event-driven wake-up receivers for body-area networks. [SYN-PUB-09637, SYN-PUB-09629, SYN-PUB-07802]

### Risks and constraints
- The published duty-cycle scheduling work covers IEEE 802.15.4; the company's radio stack is BLE on nRF52840. [SYN-PUB-09632, C005]
- The researcher's profile states limited bandwidth until mid-2025. [R0905]
- The compressed-sensing work covers sparse signal reconstruction in wireless body-area networks; C005-CH1 states the 250 Hz sampling rate cannot drop. [SYN-PUB-09636, C005-CH1]

- Data access: Kardiolink provides the master's student with access to the reference hardware and measurement equipment on-site for the duration of the project; raw current-profile data stays on company premises and is shared with the research team only under a signed NDA.
- Preconditions: The project starts after both parties sign a collaboration agreement covering IP ownership, data confidentiality, and the master's student's affiliation and insurance at the company site.
