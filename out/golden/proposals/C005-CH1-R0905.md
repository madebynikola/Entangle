## Duty-Cycle Scheduling and ADC Tuning to Cut KL-Patch 72 Active Current by 35%

**Company:** Kardiolink a.d. works in medtech and digital health. [C005] Challenge C005-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Filip Mijatović is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Power, Electronics and Telecommunications, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0905]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
The KL-Patch 72 consumes 18 mAh per day when Bluetooth is active, leaving a 54 mAh cell with barely enough capacity for the full 72-hour wear period in active patients. [C005-CH1] Kardiolink has already tuned BLE advertising intervals, moved to BLE 5.0 long-range PHY, and switched to a lower-power MCU variant, and is now within a few milliamps of what can be achieved through firmware changes alone. [C005-CH1] Kardiolink suspects remaining headroom lies in radio duty cycling, ADC configuration, and memory access patterns. [C005-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2021 study reports an adaptive IoMT ECG node that dynamically manages hardware and software configuration at runtime to minimise power consumption on a resource-constrained microcontroller. [W3171699581]
- A 2024 taxonomy identifies low-power techniques for wearable physiological monitoring devices and proposes strategies for their combination to minimise power consumption. [W4401326590]

### Research questions
1. Can deadline-aware BLE connection-event scheduling on the nRF52840 reduce radio active time by ≥35% compared to the current firmware baseline, measured as mean active current in mA from Keysight N6705C profiles over 72-hour runs, without letting ECG sampling drop below 250 Hz?
2. Which ADC operating-mode configuration (sampling rate, resolution, oversampling, sleep re-entry timing) on the nRF52840 minimises front-end current contribution while holding ECG signal quality above the clinical threshold, measured as SNDR against the reference capture at 250 Hz?
3. Does combining the optimised radio schedule with the tuned ADC configuration achieve a total active-current draw below 11.7 mAh per day (≥35% below the 18 mAh baseline), measured end-to-end on instrumented reference hardware?

### Approach
- Profile the KL-Patch 72 firmware at subsystem level using the Keysight N6705C to assign the 18 mAh daily budget across radio, ADC, MCU core, and memory, establishing which subsystems offer the most reduction headroom before any change is made.
- Apply deadline-aware slot allocation, drawn from Mijatović's duty-cycled MAC scheduling work, to BLE connection-event timing on the nRF52840: calculate the tightest interval at which ECG packets can be delivered within their deadline, then extend MCU and radio sleep between events to the maximum permitted by the 250 Hz sampling constraint.
- Tune nRF52840 ADC operating modes — resolution, oversampling factor, and sleep re-entry timing — guided by Mijatović's mixed-signal ECG front-end design experience, verifying that SNDR at 250 Hz is preserved after each configuration change.
- Integrate the revised radio schedule and ADC configuration into the FreeRTOS firmware, run a 72-hour current profile on instrumented hardware, and compare measured mAh per day against the 18 mAh baseline and the 11.7 mAh target.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: the company transfers a reference KL-Patch 72 unit with build environment access and the current FreeRTOS firmware to the master's student; Mijatović and the company's firmware lead agree on the profiling measurement protocol and baseline run conditions.
- Month 1: the master's student runs baseline 72-hour current profiles on the Keysight N6705C for three operating scenarios (rest, moderate activity, continuous BLE connection) to establish the subsystem budget; Mijatović reviews and signs off.
- Month 2–3: subsystem profiling is completed; Mijatović and the firmware lead hold a joint session to rank the three candidate areas (radio duty cycle, ADC configuration, memory access) by reduction potential and set the optimisation order.
- Month 4–6: the master's student implements the first BLE scheduling change, the company's firmware engineers review and merge into a test branch, and the first post-change 72-hour profile is taken and compared against the baseline.

### Why this pair
The KL-Patch 72 problem sits at the intersection of BLE scheduling and mixed-signal ADC configuration — exactly where Mijatović's published work on deadline-aware duty cycling and ECG front-end design meets a real device with measured current profiles. The company brings the instrumented hardware, the Keysight analyser, and a firmware team that can integrate and validate changes; Mijatović brings the scheduling method and the analogue design knowledge needed to tune the ADC without compromising ECG quality — Kardiolink brings the instrumented hardware, current profiles, and a five-engineer firmware team; Mijatović brings duty-cycled scheduling methods and mixed-signal front-end design knowledge from published work.
