## System-Level AFE and Radio Duty-Cycle Optimisation to Cut KL-Patch 72 Active Current by 35%

**Company:** Kardiolink a.d. works in medtech and digital health. [C005] Challenge C005-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Filip Mijatović is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Power, Electronics and Telecommunications, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0905]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
The KL-Patch 72 consumes 18 mAh per day when BLE is active, against a 54 mAh lithium cell, leaving insufficient margin for the full 72-hour wear period on active patients. [C005-CH1] Kardiolink must reduce active current draw by at least 35% while keeping the ECG sampling rate at 250 Hz or above to preserve clinical signal validity. [C005-CH1] The team has tuned BLE advertising intervals, adopted BLE 5.0 long-range PHY, and switched to a lower-power MCU variant, and is now within a few milliamps of what firmware changes alone can achieve; the device runs FreeRTOS on a Nordic nRF52840. [C005-CH1, C005/job/2024-02-05/Embedded Firmware Engineer — Low-Power BLE Systems] Kardiolink currently deploys approximately 2,200 KL-Patch 72 devices per month and plans to grow to 6,000 active patches per month over the following two years. [C005-CH2, C005]

### Prior work
- A 2019 study derives power-consumption models for duty-cycling schemes and determines the maximum energy budget an additional wake-up radio can consume before duty-cycling becomes more efficient in IoT networks. [W2942345566]
- A 2022 study shows that combining dynamic voltage and frequency scaling with duty-cycling on an MSP430-based sensor node significantly reduces normalised power while sustaining throughput in high-data IoT applications. [W4311857643]

### Research questions
1. Can event-driven radio wake-up techniques — adapted from 868 MHz body-area network designs to the nRF52840's BLE radio — reduce the radio's share of active current by at least 35%, measured by before-and-after current profiles on the Keysight N6705C across a continuous 72-hour recording session at 250 Hz?
2. Can board-level analogue front-end redesign, guided by sub-1 µA biopotential amplifier principles, reduce the AFE's quiescent current contribution sufficiently to meet the residual current budget after radio optimisation, measured against the current profile of the unmodified patch at 250 Hz sampling?

### Approach
- Using the Keysight N6705C, profile the current draw of each subsystem — BLE radio, nRF52840 MCU core, and analogue front-end — across a simulated 72-hour session at 250 Hz to establish the per-block budget and identify where the ≥35% headroom must come from. This step uses the instrumented reference hardware Kardiolink already has in the lab.
- Apply the wake-up receiver and deadline-aware MAC scheduling framework from the researcher's body-area network work to redesign the radio access pattern on the nRF52840: replace always-on BLE advertising with an event-triggered connection scheme that keeps the radio off between ECG events, while the deadline-aware scheduler ensures no 250 Hz sample window is missed.
- Guided by the sub-1 µA instrumentation-amplifier design principles from the researcher's ECG/EEG acquisition work, audit and modify the board-level AFE — bias currents, supply rail, and signal-chain topology — to reduce quiescent current without altering the 250 Hz signal path or clinical bandwidth.
- Validate the combined radio and AFE changes against the ≥35% active-current reduction target using repeated 72-hour current profiles on the N6705C, and confirm ECG signal quality at 250 Hz against recordings from the unmodified patch, documenting which interventions account for which share of the saving.

### Why this pair
Kardiolink has exhausted firmware-level optimisation and now needs system-level analysis of the radio duty-cycle architecture and the analogue front-end current budget — two hardware-domain problems that require specialist circuit and protocol knowledge the firmware team does not cover. Filip Mijatović brings exactly those two bodies of work: event-driven wake-up receiver design for body-area networks, and sub-1 µA biopotential front-end design for ECG acquisition. Neither side could reach the ≥35% target alone: Kardiolink brings the instrumented device, the measurement bench, and the firmware integration capability; the researcher brings the radio duty-cycling and AFE design methods that can find the remaining headroom in the hardware stack.

- Kardiolink is within a few milliamps of what firmware changes alone can achieve, and is seeking expertise in radio duty cycling and ADC configuration to find the remaining headroom. [C005-CH1]
- Filip Mijatović co-authored work on wake-up receiver circuits at 4.2 µW for body-area networks and co-authored work on deadline-aware MAC scheduling achieving below 0.3% deadline miss rates at up to 80% superframe utilisation. [SYN-PUB-07802, SYN-PUB-09632]
- The researcher co-authored work on sub-1 µA ECG/EEG instrumentation amplifiers and led the SENSEC project developing analogue front-ends and authenticated wake-up protocols for wearable cardiac monitoring, with a pilot recording campaign at a partner hospital cardiology ward to establish clinical relevance. [SYN-PUB-09637, SYN-PUB-09629, SYN-PRJ-0211]
