## Morphology-Aware Query Expansion and Script Normalization for Serbian Support Search

**Company:** LinguaBot d.o.o. works in conversational AI. [C009] Challenge C009-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Stefan Dragić is a Full Professor in the Department of Mathematics and Informatics, Faculty of Sciences. [R0349]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
LinguaBot's Elasticsearch system returns no useful result for 18% of 12,000 real support queries sampled from one bank client. [C009-CH1] More than half of missed queries are caused by morphological mismatch or script confusion: Serbian inflects heavily (kartica, karticu, karticom) and users mix Cyrillic and Latin mid-sentence. [C009-CH1] The deployed stemmer is a Croatian stemmer paired with a simple script-normalisation step, and the system struggles with Serbian morphology and mixed-script input. [C009-CH1] Clients add new product terminology every quarter; the system must handle additions without full re-indexing, and the target is to keep the miss rate below 5%. [C009-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2023 survey of stemming methods for inflected languages finds that linguistic knowledge-based techniques were widely used for highly inflected languages and reported higher accuracy than other techniques. [W4388666591]
- A 2020 study presents a sequence-to-sequence lemmatizer evaluated on 52 languages that reduces lemmatization errors by 19% on average over the prior best system. [W2911610808]

### Research questions
1. Does replacing the Croatian stemmer with a phoneme-based script-normalisation layer and Serbian morphological query expansion reduce the miss rate on LinguaBot's 85,000-pair dataset from 18% to below 5%, measured as the proportion of queries returning no useful result at rank 1–3?
2. Does an incremental indexing approach using streaming re-normalisation keep the miss rate below 5% after each quarterly terminology update, measured on a held-out set of 500 new-term queries per update cycle?
3. Does domain-adapted query expansion—trained on financial and telecom vocabulary from the annotated dataset—outperform a general Serbian morphological analyser on the same 85,000-pair benchmark, measured by mean reciprocal rank?

### Approach
- Replace the Croatian stemmer with a phoneme-based canonicalization layer drawn from the researcher's script-normalisation work, so that Cyrillic and Latin spellings of the same word map to a single index token regardless of mid-sentence script switches.
- Wrap the canonicalization layer with query expansion: at query time, generate transliteration variants and morphological forms (stemmed and lemmatized) using the researcher's transliteration-variant expansion method, then pass all variants to Elasticsearch as a disjunction query.
- Adapt the expansion tables to financial and telecom vocabulary by running the expansion pipeline over the company's 85,000-pair annotated dataset, with the company's two computational linguists validating domain-specific forms each iteration.
- Plug the updated pipeline into a streaming incremental indexing architecture so new terminology added each quarter triggers normalisation and expansion of only the new documents, with no full re-index required.

### Why this pair
LinguaBot has a precisely measured problem—18% miss rate, a clear domain (financial and telecom support), a large annotated benchmark, and two linguists who can validate outputs—but lacks the script-normalisation and morphological expansion methods needed to go below 5%. Stefan Dragić brings exactly those methods (phoneme-based canonicalization, transliteration-variant query expansion, streaming incremental indexing for Serbian), developed and published for mixed-script Serbian retrieval, plus prior experience delivering a custom search module for an industry partner. Neither could do it alone: the company brings the domain data, the live Elasticsearch environment, and the linguistic validators; Stefan Dragić brings the normalisation and expansion methods; the day-to-day implementation work could be carried out by a master's student or junior researcher from the faculty group, subject to agreement at project start.
