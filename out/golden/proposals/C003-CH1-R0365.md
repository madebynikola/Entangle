## Thermo-Mechanical Fatigue Characterisation of Weld Joints in Cold-Store Conveyor Frames

**Company:** AutomVoj a.d. works in robotics and industrial automation. [C003] Challenge C003-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Bojan Erić is an Associate Professor in the Department of Mechanical Engineering (Machine Design and Mechanics), Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0365]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
Mild steel conveyor frames crack at crossmember-to-stringer weld joints after roughly 18 months of continuous operation in food-processing cold stores at 2–6 °C, and AutomVoj has replaced 14 assemblies under warranty this year. [C003-CH1] The company has tested frames under load at room temperature and cannot reproduce the failure, and suspects that thermal cycling, condensation, and vibration interact to cause the cracking. [C003-CH1] The company has photographs, measurements from site visits, and two failed frame sections with visible weld cracks available for investigation. [C003-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2024 study reports that the crack growth rate of welded steel joints in a corrosive environment is higher than that of base metal, driven by residual stress and high dislocation density in the heat-affected zone. [W4402945203]

### Research questions
1. Does the crossmember-to-stringer weld joint in mild steel conveyor frames initiate cracks under combined low-temperature thermal cycling (2–6 °C operating range) and cyclic mechanical loading at frequencies representative of conveyor vibration? Measured by crack initiation life and crack opening displacement on specimens machined from the company's failed frames and from newly fabricated reference joints, tested in the climate chamber at low temperature.
2. Does residual stress in the weld toe — measured by X-ray diffraction and hole-drilling on the failed sections and modelled by finite element simulation with kinematic hardening — concentrate stress intensity to a level that explains the observed 18-month crack initiation period? Measured against fracture mechanics thresholds computed using XFEM for the joint geometry.
3. Can a change to welding procedure, filler material, or joint geometry reduce the weld-toe stress intensity factor by a measurable margin under the same combined loading conditions? Measured by comparing crack initiation life and residual stress level between the current joint and candidate improved joints in climate-chamber tests.

### Approach
- Fractographic and metallographic analysis of the two failed frame sections: map crack morphology, identify initiation sites at the weld toe or root, and characterise HAZ microstructure using Charpy and CTOD sub-size specimens at sub-zero temperature, following the method used in Erić's fracture-toughness characterisation of HAZ at sub-zero temperatures.
- Residual stress measurement on the failed sections and on reference weld joints fabricated to the company's current welding procedure: hole-drilling for through-thickness profile and X-ray diffraction for surface stress; finite element simulation with kinematic hardening models residual stress redistribution under cyclic loading, following the methods in Erić's published work on residual stress in welded joints.
- Combined thermo-mechanical fatigue testing on weld-joint specimens in the climate chamber: thermal cycling across the cold-store operating range, with simultaneous cyclic mechanical loading representative of conveyor vibration amplitude; digital image correlation monitors crack closure and full-field strain at the weld toe, following the experimental protocol from Erić's thermo-mechanical fatigue studies.
- XFEM computation of stress intensity factors at the weld toe for the current joint geometry and for candidate improved geometries; results from the residual stress measurements are used as initial conditions; candidates showing a reduced stress intensity factor are fabricated to the company's welding capability and re-tested in the climate chamber.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: AutomVoj ships the two failed frame sections and transfers welding procedure records, filler material data sheets, and vibration amplitude measurements from site visits to the faculty laboratory.
- Week 3–6: Erić's master's student carries out fractographic and metallographic examination and machines sub-zero Charpy and CTOD specimens from the HAZ of the failed sections;
- Month 2: Hole-drilling and X-ray diffraction residual stress measurements on the failed sections, carried out in the faculty laboratory by the master's student; Erić reviews and interprets results.
- Month 3: Climate chamber and servo-hydraulic machine time is booked (advance coordination required); reference weld joints are fabricated by the company to the current procedure and shipped to the faculty for combined thermo-mechanical fatigue testing starting in month 4.

### Why this pair
AutomVoj has the failed hardware, the fabrication capability, and the operational context, but lacks low-temperature fatigue testing equipment and the fracture mechanics methods needed to reproduce and diagnose the failure. Erić's group has the climate chamber, servo-hydraulic machine, digital image correlation setup, and a published track record of exactly the combined thermo-mechanical fatigue and residual stress work the diagnosis requires — Together they cover every element of the investigation: neither side could reproduce the failure, characterise its mechanism, and produce implementable design recommendations without the other.

- AutomVoj cannot reproduce the failure at room temperature; testing must occur at low temperature under thermal cycling, which requires equipment the company does not have. [C003-CH1]
- Erić co-authored studies of thermo-mechanical fatigue in welded structures under thermal cycling between −30 °C and +20 °C and has access to a climate chamber reaching −60 °C and a servo-hydraulic fatigue machine. [SYN-PUB-04475, SYN-PUB-04482, R0365]
- Erić co-authored work on residual stress measurement in welded joints and XFEM fracture mechanics analysis of weld-toe cracks under combined loading. [SYN-PUB-04473, SYN-PUB-04478, SYN-PUB-04477]

### Risks and constraints
- The climate chamber and servo-hydraulic machines are shared equipment and require advance coordination for extended campaigns (R0365). [R0365]
- The records cover fatigue and fracture under mechanical and thermal cycling; the corrosion-assisted component from condensation is not addressed in the published work. [SYN-PUB-04482, SYN-PUB-04475, SYN-PUB-04474]

### Agreed up front (proposed)
- Publication: results may be submitted for publication after a 60-day written review period by AutomVoj; the company may request redaction of specific proprietary weld procedure details.
- Ownership: AutomVoj owns the test data, the calibrated finite element model, and any recommendations for welding procedure or joint design changes; the Faculty of Technical Sciences retains the right to use the experimental methods and general findings in future academic work.
- Data access: AutomVoj transfers the two failed frame sections, welding procedure records, filler material data sheets, and site vibration measurements to the faculty laboratory within two weeks of project start; data is handled under a signed confidentiality agreement.
- Preconditions: the climate chamber and servo-hydraulic machine must be booked at least four weeks in advance; the project start date is contingent on confirmed equipment availability for months 4–6.
