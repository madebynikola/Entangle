## Low-Temperature Thermo-Mechanical Fatigue Analysis of Weld Cracking in Chilled Conveyor Frames

**Company:** AutomVoj a.d. works in robotics and industrial automation. [C003] Challenge C003-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Bojan Erić is an Associate Professor in the Department of Mechanical Engineering (Machine Design and Mechanics), Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0365]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
Welded mild steel conveyor frames are cracking at the main crossmember-to-stringer connections after approximately 18 months of chilled operation at 2–6 °C, but the same frames pass load testing at room temperature in the workshop. [C003-CH1] AutomVoj has replaced 14 frame assemblies under warranty this year; two failed sections remain in storage with site-visit photos and measurements. [C003-CH1] The company suspects thermal cycling, condensation, and vibration are interacting to cause the failure, but cannot reproduce or characterise the phenomenon at ambient workshop conditions. [C003-CH1] The workshop does not have equipment or know-how for low-temperature cyclic load testing. [C003-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2023 study reports that welding residual stress significantly increases the effective crack driving force and reduces fatigue life, with the fatigue life at the weld toe without considering residual stress being approximately twice that when residual stress is considered. [W4388490665]

### Research questions
1. Can thermo-mechanical cycling at 2–6 °C under representative conveyor vibration loads reproduce the observed weld-toe cracking mode in laboratory specimens cut from MIG/MAG welded mild steel hollow sections? Measured by crack location, morphology, and depth against fractographic evidence from the two failed field sections.
2. Do welding residual stresses at the crossmember-to-stringer joint, measured by X-ray diffraction on the failed sections and on fresh specimens, reach levels that — combined with service loads — exceed the fatigue crack growth threshold at 2–6 °C? Measured by comparing effective stress intensity factor ranges to the material's low-temperature threshold from Charpy and CTOD tests.
3. Which single mechanism or combination — thermal fatigue, residual-stress-assisted crack growth, or corrosion-fatigue from condensation — governs failure life? Measured by rank-ordering predicted crack growth lives from each mechanism model against the observed 18-month field life.

### Approach
- Cut representative weld specimens from the two failed sections and from fresh MIG/MAG welded hollow-section joints fabricated to AutomVoj's procedure. Perform fractographic examination and hardness mapping to characterise crack initiation sites and heat-affected zone microstructure.
- Measure residual stresses on the failed and fresh specimens by X-ray diffraction, following the method Erić co-authored for weld residual stress measurement. Build a finite element model to compute stress intensity factor ranges at the weld toe under combined residual stress and service loading, using the sequentially coupled thermal–structural and Chaboche cyclic-plasticity modelling approaches from Erić's co-authored FE work on residual stress in welds.
- Apply thermo-mechanical fatigue cycling in the climate chamber at 2–6 °C with superimposed cyclic mechanical loads representative of conveyor vibration, monitoring crack closure and crack opening displacement by digital image correlation, following the method co-authored for combined thermal and mechanical cycling.
- Assess low-temperature fracture toughness of the weld heat-affected zone by Charpy impact and CTOD testing at 2–6 °C following the method co-authored for HAZ microstructures down to −60 °C. Combine all results into a failure characterisation report that identifies the governing mechanism and specifies the weld geometry or procedure change needed to prevent recurrence.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: AutomVoj transfers the two failed sections and all site-visit photos, load records, and fabrication procedure documents to the Faculty lab. The company engineer briefs Erić's group on the conveyor duty cycle and operating environment.
- Month 1: The master's student cuts weld-toe specimens from the failed sections. Erić's group performs optical and SEM fractography, Vickers hardness mapping across the HAZ, and XRD residual stress measurements on the failed joint faces.
- Month 2–3: Erić's group fabricates fresh MIG/MAG welded hollow-section specimens to AutomVoj's welding procedure. XRD measurements are taken on the fresh specimens to establish the as-welded residual stress baseline for comparison with the failed sections.
- Month 3–4: First thermo-mechanical fatigue tests run in the climate chamber at 2–6 °C; DIC is set up on the specimen surface at the weld toe. The company engineer reviews interim fractographic findings and confirms whether observed crack locations match those seen on the field failures.

### Why this pair
AutomVoj has the only primary evidence that matters — the two failed sections, the field records, and the exact fabrication procedure — but cannot generate the low-temperature cyclic loading needed to understand or reproduce the failure. Erić's group has the climate chamber, the servo-hydraulic rig, the DIC system, and published methods covering every step of the proposed investigation: low-temperature HAZ toughness, thermo-mechanical fatigue with DIC, and residual stress measurement with FE simulation. Neither side can do this alone: the company has the physical evidence and the production context; the researcher has the equipment and the tested methods to turn that evidence into a governed failure explanation and a prevention recommendation.

- AutomVoj cannot reproduce the failure at ambient conditions and has no equipment for low-temperature cyclic testing, while two failed sections with site records are in storage and ready for analysis. [C003-CH1]
- Erić co-authored publications covering low-temperature fracture toughness of weld HAZ microstructures down to −60 °C and thermo-mechanical fatigue with DIC crack monitoring. [SYN-PUB-04476, SYN-PUB-04482, SYN-PUB-04475]
- Erić offers the servo-hydraulic fatigue machine, climate chamber to −60 °C, and DIC setup that the company's workshop does not have. [R0365, C003-CH1]

### Risks and constraints
- The publications cover structural steels and welds in general; it is not confirmed that the record covers MIG/MAG welded mild steel hollow sections specifically as used in C003 conveyor frames. [SYN-PUB-04476, SYN-PUB-04480, C003]
