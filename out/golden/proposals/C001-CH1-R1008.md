## Unsupervised On-Device Anomaly Detection for Site-Specific Modbus and BACnet Traffic

**Company:** ArmaturaSec d.o.o. works in cybersecurity. [C001] Challenge C001-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Krisztina Kiss is a Full Professor in the Department of Computing and Control Engineering, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R1008]  
**Format:** industrial PhD, three years. The researcher is the academic supervisor; the company names an industrial supervisor and employs the candidate.

### The problem
ProtoSense is supposed to flag anomalous behaviour, but supervised classifiers trained on public ICS datasets produced unacceptable false positive rates in real deployments, leaving the detection problem unsolved. [C001-CH1, C001/blog/2024-05-20/Why We Stopped Trying to Use Public ICS Datasets for Anomaly Detection] ArmaturaSec has several terabytes of normal Modbus and BACnet traffic from consenting clients over three years but no confirmed malicious examples, making supervised labelling impossible. [C001-CH1] Any anomaly flag must be explained to plant managers in plain language; opaque scores are not acceptable. [C001-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2018 study reports that a deep CNN trained only on unlabelled time-series data can detect point anomalies, contextual anomalies, and discords without requiring labelled attack examples. [W2906498146]

### Research questions
1. Can a one-class detector trained solely on site-captured Modbus and BACnet traffic, using byte-level payload features, achieve a false positive rate below the threshold observed with public-dataset-trained classifiers, measured on ArmaturaSec's multi-site traffic corpus with operator-verified normal labels?
2. Can a quantised and structurally pruned version of the detector run on the fifth hardware variant's security processor without increasing packet forwarding latency, measured by latency benchmarks on the target board under full forwarding load?
3. Does incremental online learning with Bayesian updates keep the detector's false positive rate stable when site-normal behaviour shifts, measured by monitoring flag rates before and after five controlled configuration changes injected into the lab testbed traffic?

### Approach
- Characterise Modbus and BACnet traffic in ArmaturaSec's corpus using byte-level payload feature extraction and periodicity analysis, extending the protocol-agnostic feature pipeline from the researcher's one-class classification work to cover BACnet function codes and timing patterns not previously studied.
- Train and evaluate one-class detectors — including autoencoder ensembles and one-class SVMs — on site-specific captures, using threshold calibration on unlabelled operational data with beta mixture models so that no labelled attacks are needed at any stage.
- Compress the best-performing detector via quantisation and structured pruning of its autoencoder, targeting deployment on the fifth hardware variant's security processor, and benchmark latency under forwarding load to confirm the hard constraint is met.
- Add incremental online learning with Gaussian mixture models and Bayesian updates so that the deployed model adapts when a plant's normal polling rhythm changes, and pair each flag with a plain-language explanation derived from the feature dimension responsible for the highest reconstruction error.

### First six months
- Month 1–2: The company engineer transfers a representative sample of Modbus and BACnet captures to the researcher's testbed under a data-sharing agreement. The candidate sets up the ingestion and feature-extraction pipeline and documents BACnet function code distribution across site types.
- Month 2–3: The researcher and candidate review operator logs at two client sites to agree on what counts as normal; these become the reference labels for all evaluation. The company engineer provides site context.
- Month 3–5: The candidate trains initial one-class detectors on Modbus traffic from two sites and measures false positive rates against the reference labels, establishing the baseline the rest of the PhD improves on.
- Month 5–6: The candidate runs the same pipeline on BACnet captures and reports where BACnet behaviour differs from Modbus in ways that affect feature choice, producing a short technical note that guides Year 1 dataset work.

### Why this pair
The company holds a rare, large corpus of real operational traffic but hit a research-grade obstacle — unsupervised site-specific baseline modelling — that its engineer cannot resolve alone from background reading. The researcher brings the exact combination of methods the problem demands: one-class detection without labels, compression for constrained hardware, and drift-adaptive online learning, all demonstrated on ICS traffic. Neither could do it alone: ArmaturaSec brings several terabytes of site-specific Modbus and BACnet traffic from consenting clients, a bench with physical boards and PLC simulators, and an engineer who has been reading about anomaly detection for six months; the researcher brings the methods, the ICS testbed for controlled experiments, and the supervision to turn three years of work into a deployable system.
