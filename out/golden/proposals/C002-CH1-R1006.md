## Detecting Random Guessing in Primary-School Mathematics Game Clickstream Logs

**Company:** Zrenjanin Game Works d.o.o. works in game development. [C002] Challenge C002-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Danilo Mitrović is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Mathematics and Informatics, Faculty of Sciences. [R1006]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
Approximately 4,200 students' clickstream logs from the 2022–23 and 2023–24 school years record timestamp, question ID, answer chosen, time-on-question, and attempt count for every interaction, but the company has no method to separate guessing from genuine learning in that data. [C002-CH1, C002/job/2024-06-01/Freelance Data Analyst — Learning Outcomes Project (Short-Term Contract)] Teachers report that high in-game scores do not always reflect understanding on paper tests, and the company acknowledges that students who click fast can achieve high scores without understanding the material. [C002-CH1, C002/blog/2024-09-10/What We Learned at the Novi Sad Education Technology Gathering] The company needs a testable method to flag probable guessers before the 2025–26 school year so teachers can intervene early. [C002-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2019 study reports a hierarchical mixture model that jointly identifies random guessing and item-level non-response from response times and answer patterns at the item-by-examinee level. [W2983537616]
- A 2020 study reports that combining response-time thresholds with response behaviours improves detection of rapid disengagement compared to time thresholds alone. [W3029100084]

### Research questions
1. Can session-level features engineered from time-on-question and attempt-count sequences — processed by a hidden Markov model — identify a rapid-guessing latent state that predicts low paper-test scores? Measured as precision and recall of the guesser flag against teacher assessments on a held-out subset of students from the 2023–24 data.
2. Can students be segmented into stable behavioural profiles (e.g., effortful, routine, rapid-guessing) using latent profile analysis on the engineered features, and do profile assignments match teacher-reported understanding? Measured as agreement rate between profile membership and teacher classification on the same held-out subset.
3. Can the detection rules be reduced to a small, interpretable decision table — using at most four feature thresholds — that classroom teachers can apply without statistical tools? Measured by a usability check with two teachers from the Zrenjanin and Subotica schools.

### Approach
- Engineer session-level features from the CSV logs: median and variance of time-on-question per session, first-attempt accuracy rate, ratio of minimum-time responses to total responses, and attempt-count distribution. These map directly onto the time-on-question, answer, and attempt-count fields in the company's data.
- Apply a hidden Markov model to each student's sequence of per-question feature vectors to estimate the probability of being in a rapid-guessing latent state at each question. This follows the researcher's published method for detecting latent states and transitions in longitudinal clickstream sequences.
- Use longitudinal latent profile analysis on session-aggregated features to segment students into behavioural archetypes across the two school years, following the researcher's published approach for tracking profile transitions. Profiles are described in plain language for teachers.
- Distil the HMM and profile outputs into an explainable decision table — threshold rules on two to four features — validated against teacher assessments. The table is the deliverable teachers use; the models are the derivation tool.

### First six months
- Month 1: the company exports the full CSV logs from MySQL and shares them with the research team under a signed data-sharing agreement; the master's student sets up the analysis environment and runs an initial data-quality audit.
- Month 2: the master's student and Mitrović define the feature set (time-on-question statistics, first-attempt accuracy, minimum-time-response ratio, attempt-count distribution) and compute it for all students; the company's lead developer confirms the feature definitions match the log schema.
- Month 3: Mitrović and the master's student fit a baseline threshold classifier (response-time cutoff only, following the published rapid-disengagement literature) to give a reference point for the HMM and latent profile analysis results.
- Months 4–6: fit, validate, and interpret the HMM and latent profile models; produce the decision table; run the teacher usability check with support from the company's pedagogy contact.

### Why this pair
The company has two years of fine-grained interaction logs and direct access to teachers who can validate flags, but no method to extract guessing signals from those logs and no in-house expertise to build one. Mitrović brings the specific methods the problem requires — hidden Markov models for latent behavioural states and latent profile analysis for student segmentation — applied to clickstream data. Neither could do it alone; Mitrović brings the modelling methods and a master's student to do the day-to-day analysis work within the researcher's reduced-capacity window.
