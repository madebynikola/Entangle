## Latent Profile and Sequence Clustering to Flag Guessing in Game-Based Learning Clickstreams

**Company:** Zrenjanin Game Works d.o.o. works in game development. [C002] Challenge C002-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Danilo Mitrović is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Mathematics and Informatics, Faculty of Sciences. [R1006]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
The platform logs clickstream events for 4,200 students across two school years; one teacher reported a student with a 91% score who failed a paper-based test on the same material, suggesting the score reflected guessing rather than learning. [C002-CH1] The logged fields — question ID, answer chosen, timestamp, attempt count, and time-on-question — are what the company has, and the company states it has no way to read anything meaningful out of them. [C002-CH1] The company needs a method to flag students likely guessing so teachers can intervene. [C002-CH1]

### Prior work
- A 2023 study reports that ML and LSTM models detect student cheating behaviour from behavioural logs with 90% accuracy, using a dataset combining surveys, sensor data, and institutional records. [W4366773002]

### Research questions
1. Can latent profile analysis and sequence clustering on attempt-count and time-on-question trajectories identify a distinct behavioural profile consistent with random clicking — measured by whether profile membership predicts paper-test failure across a held-out subset of the 4,200 students?
2. Does a logistic regression model trained on quiz-attempt features (attempts per question, response-time variance, answer-switch rate) flag guessing students at a precision and recall acceptable to teachers — measured against teacher-labelled cases gathered in the first pilot school?

### Approach
- Apply latent profile analysis and hidden Markov models to each student's attempt-count and time-on-question sequences to discover discrete behavioural profiles; one or more profiles are hypothesised to match random-clicking patterns. This follows the longitudinal latent variable modelling approach used by Mitrović on LMS clickstream data.
- Cluster answer-attempt sequences using dynamic time warping and k-medoids to group students with similar trajectory shapes, providing a second, model-free view of engagement patterns that can cross-validate the latent profiles.
- Train a logistic regression model on quiz-attempt features — chosen for its interpretable coefficients — to produce a per-student guessing-risk score. Quiz-attempt features have been shown to be the most portable across courses in Mitrović's prior work, making them suitable for the varied game modules in the platform.
- Translate the model output into a teacher-facing report: a ranked class list with a plain-language risk label and the two or three feature values that drove each student's score, requiring no statistical knowledge to read.

### First six months
- Month 1 — data transfer: the company exports anonymised CSVs for both school years and delivers them to the master's student under a signed data-use agreement; Mitrović's group sets up a secure analysis environment.
- Month 1–2 — feature definition: Mitrović and the company's pedagogy contact (one day per week) agree on the three to five behavioural features to extract and draft a teacher-labelling protocol for the pilot school.
- Month 2–3 — profile discovery: the master's student runs latent profile analysis and HMM fitting; Mitrović reviews the profiles and selects the best-fitting solution for presentation to the company.
- Month 4–5 — teacher labelling: the company's pedagogy contact coordinates with two or three pilot-school teachers to label a sample of students; the master's student trains and evaluates the logistic regression model on those labels.

### Why this pair
The company holds two years of clickstream logs from 4,200 students and has teacher relationships that can supply ground-truth labels, but has no method to construct behavioural profiles from those logs or build a validated flag model. Mitrović brings latent variable modelling of clickstream sequences, trajectory clustering, and portable quiz-attempt prediction, along with prior experience advising on the design of a learner progress scoring system for an e-learning platform. Neither could do this alone: the company brings the data, the domain context, and the teacher access needed to label and pilot the output; Mitrović's group brings the methods and the analytical capacity to build, validate, and document a model the company can operate year on year.

### Risks and constraints
- The records do not include work on guessing or disengaged-responding detection specifically; the clustering and prediction work covers dropout and performance, which is a related but distinct outcome from random-click detection. [SYN-PUB-10454, SYN-PUB-10455, SYN-PUB-10457]
- The output must be explainable and actionable for classroom teachers without statistical training (C002/job/2024-06-01); the researcher's prediction work uses gradient-boosted trees and LSTM neural networks (SYN-PUB-10457, SYN-PUB-10458). - Publication: results may be submitted for publication after a 30-day review by the company; the company may request removal of commercially sensitive details but may not block publication on scientific grounds.
