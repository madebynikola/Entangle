## Kriging-Based Fusion of Gauge and Satellite Data to Correct Parametric Crop-Insurance Triggers

**Company:** OsiguroMi d.o.o. works in insurtech — embedded micro-insurance and parametric products. [C004] Challenge C004-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Milutin Đukić is a Principal Research Fellow in the Remote Sensing and GIS Group, BioSense Institute. [R0727]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
OsiguroMi's parametric crop insurance pays out when the nearest official station records rainfall below a contracted threshold, but that station is sometimes 30 km from the insured plot and separated from it by terrain features that substantially alter local rainfall. [C004-CH1] Partners in Vojvodina and Bačka report that payouts were triggered when fields had not dried out, and were withheld when crops had already been lost — direct evidence of basis risk. [C004-CH1] The company has access to RHMZS data and three private agrometeorological networks totalling around 140 measurement points in the region, and has considered satellite soil-moisture indices, but is uncertain how to combine these sources into a single reliable trigger. [C004-CH1] OsiguroMi plans to expand the agro line to Bulgaria and Romania by the end of 2025 through partnerships with regional cooperatives. [C004]

### Prior work
- A 2021 study assessed ordinary kriging (OK) and ordinary cokriging (OCK) for spatial interpolation of quarterly and monthly precipitation in Poland, incorporating SMOS satellite-derived soil moisture data alongside rain-gauge station data. [W3137160051]
- A 2022 study reports that an optimised kriging approach interpolating relative rainfall anomalies at 250 m resolution over a 30-year record achieves R² = 0.78 in leave-one-out cross-validation against 622 stations, though with a known smoothing effect at high-rainfall extremes. [W4213059839]

### Research questions
1. Can a co-kriging model that combines the ~140-point gauge network with satellite soil-moisture indices produce per-plot rainfall estimates in Vojvodina and Bačka that reduce payout errors compared with single-station triggers? Measured as the change in the rate of mismatched payouts against the company's historical partner feedback dataset.
2. Which kriging variant — ordinary kriging, regression-kriging, or co-kriging with satellite soil moisture — achieves the lowest cross-validation error on the company's gauge network for the rainfall events associated with recorded claims? Measured by leave-one-out root mean square error and bias across events in the historical PostgreSQL time series.
3. Can the best-performing spatial rainfall estimate be expressed as a correction factor on the contracted station threshold — preserving the trigger structure — that a rules engine can apply automatically? Measured by the fraction of historical mismatched payouts resolved under the corrected trigger without altering payouts for correctly triggered events.

### Approach
- Apply ordinary kriging and co-kriging with satellite soil-moisture as a co-variable to the ~140-point gauge network to produce gridded daily rainfall estimates at plot level for Vojvodina and Bačka, following the approach Đukić has published for sparse climate station networks.
- Use wet-day indicator kriging, as previously applied by Đukić for binary precipitation mapping in complex terrain, to identify which plots received rainfall above zero on days when the contracted station recorded dry conditions — directly targeting the false-payout problem.
- Derive a station-to-plot correction factor by comparing kriged plot-level estimates with the contracted station reading for each historical claim event in the company's PostgreSQL time series, then express this as a multiplicative or additive offset that the existing rules engine can apply without changing the threshold structure.
- Validate the corrected trigger against partner feedback records from Vojvodina and Bačka using leave-one-out cross-validation on gauge stations, reporting reduction in basis risk events and ensuring no correctly triggered payout is reversed.

### First six months
- Month 1 — data transfer: the company's internal researcher exports the PostgreSQL gauge time series and the partner feedback records (payout vs. field outcome) to a shared secure environment; Đukić's group documents the satellite soil-moisture index sources and confirms data access.
- Month 1–2 — event labelling: the master's student aligns gauge readings, satellite indices, and claim outcomes into a single table of rainfall events, flagging each as correctly triggered, false positive, or false negative against the partner feedback.
- Month 2–3 — baseline kriging: the master's student runs ordinary kriging on the ~140-point network for all labelled events and maps the station-to-plot rainfall difference; Đukić reviews variogram fits and flags terrain-driven outliers for the co-kriging step.
- Month 3–6 — co-kriging and correction factor: the master's student runs co-kriging with satellite soil moisture and wet-day indicator kriging; derives and validates correction factors; the company's two senior engineers integrate the correction layer into the rules engine for back-testing.

### Why this pair
The company is uncertain how to reliably combine its data sources into a single trigger, and its attempted threshold correction based on historical differences between stations and terrain was a rough approximation. Đukić brings co-kriging with auxiliary co-variables and wet-day indicator kriging for binary precipitation mapping; OsiguroMi brings the ~140-point gauge network, satellite soil-moisture indices, historical loss time series, and partner feedback on payout outcomes — inputs the interpolation methods require but that sit entirely on the company's side. Together they can deliver a correction layer in six months that neither could produce alone.

### Agreed up front (proposed)
- Publication: the researcher's group may publish results after a 30-day written review by OsiguroMi; the company may request redaction of commercially sensitive threshold parameters before submission.
- Ownership: OsiguroMi owns the correction-factor model, trained variogram parameters, and any integrated rules-engine code; BioSense retains the interpolation methods and may reuse them in future research.
- Data access: the company provides gauge time series, claim records, and partner feedback to the BioSense team under a signed non-disclosure agreement; data are used solely for this project and deleted or returned at project close.
- Preconditions: the project starts only after the data-sharing agreement is signed and the company confirms that the PostgreSQL export covers at least two full growing seasons of claim events.
