## RSM-guided pyrolysis optimisation to reach 70% fixed carbon on existing batch reactor

**Company:** Suncokret Eko d.o.o. works in cleantech — biomass and organic waste valorisation. [C007] Challenge C007-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Đorđe Vukelić is an Associate Professor in the Department of Applied and Engineering Chemistry, Faculty of Technology. [R0992]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
Suncokret Eko's batch pyrolysis of sunflower husks and maize cobs runs at 500–520°C, producing biochar with 55% fixed carbon at 28% mass yield. [C007-CH1] Customers in Germany and the Netherlands require 70% fixed carbon for soil-amendment certification; raising temperature to 650°C meets the threshold but drops mass yield below 20%. [C007-CH1] A mass yield below 20% requires 40% more feedstock per order, eliminating the price premium the certified product commands. [C007-CH1] Feedstock arrives from different suppliers with variable moisture and particle size, adding process variability the company has not yet quantified. [C007-CH3]

### Prior work
- A 2023 study reports an RSM model that predicts biochar yield from fixed carbon content, reaction temperature, and heating rate, developed on a Box-Behnken design across three biomass types. [W4319953064]

### Research questions
1. Can a response-surface model, fitted to controlled production trials on the existing reactor, identify temperature–residence-time conditions that raise fixed-carbon content to at least 70% while keeping mass yield commercially viable — measured as fixed carbon and proximate analysis on each trial run, compared against the current 55%-fixed-carbon, 28%-yield baseline?
2. How much of the run-to-run variation in fixed-carbon output is explained by feedstock moisture and particle size, and can a pre-treatment or process-adjustment rule reduce that variation — measured by the coefficient of variation in fixed carbon across runs grouped by feedstock batch?

### Approach
- Map the two-year batch log to extract recorded temperature profiles, residence times, feedstock moisture, particle size, and proximate analysis results; use these to fit a preliminary response-surface model (central composite design) of fixed carbon and mass yield as functions of temperature and residence time — following the RSM and central composite design methodology from the researcher's published optimisation work.
- Run thermogravimetric analysis on representative sunflower husk and maize cob samples from current supplier batches to determine pyrolysis kinetics and conversion ranges using isoconversional methods, establishing the activation energy profile that explains the fixed-carbon plateau and informing the temperature bounds for the trial design.
- Execute a set of controlled production trials on the existing reactor at RSM-guided temperature–residence-time combinations — staying within the reactor's 400–700°C operating range — recording proximate analysis after each run; fit and validate the response-surface model to locate conditions that reach 70% fixed carbon at the highest feasible mass yield.
- Characterise the best-performing biochar against European Biochar Certificate stable-carbon criteria using the lab's proximate and ultimate analysis capability, supplemented by BET surface area and FTIR analysis at the Faculty of Technology, to confirm certification readiness.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: the company transfers the two-year batch log (temperature profiles, feedstock metadata, proximate analysis records) to the Faculty of Technology under a signed data-sharing agreement; Vukelić and a master's student audit completeness and flag missing fields.
- Week 3–6: the master's student runs TGA-DSC analysis on at least three current feedstock samples (sunflower husk and maize cob from different supplier batches) at the Faculty lab to characterise kinetics and moisture sensitivity.
- Week 7–8: Vukelić delivers a trial design matrix (central composite or Box-Behnken) specifying the temperature and residence-time combinations to be tested, confirmed against the reactor's 400–700°C operating range and the company's batch schedule.
- Week 9 onward: a company engineer begins running designed trials one or two per week, recording all process parameters; the master's student visits for sample collection and proximate analysis after each run.

### Why this pair
The company has two years of batch data and a working reactor but lacks the statistical and thermochemical methods to extract an optimal operating window from that data. Vukelić brings exactly the two methods the problem needs — response surface optimisation of the temperature–residence-time space and EBC certification characterisation — and holds a patent on the specific feedstock. Neither could do it alone: the company provides the reactor, the production trials, and the batch log that the model must be fitted to; the researcher provides the kinetic analysis, the RSM design, the characterisation lab, and a student to do the day-to-day analytical work.

- Suncokret Eko has two years of batch logs and an existing reactor but needs a method to map temperature–residence-time conditions to fixed-carbon yield without capital investment. [C007-CH1, C007]
- Vukelić co-authored published work on RSM optimisation of biochar temperature–residence-time conditions and holds a patent on certified-grade biochar production from sunflower husk. [SYN-PUB-10356, SYN-PAT-033]
- Vukelić co-authored work on EBC physicochemical certification including stable carbon content, directly matching the 70% fixed-carbon certification target. [SYN-PUB-10349]

### Risks and constraints
- The company requires a process adjustment testable within six months, involving controlled production trials on the existing reactor. [C007-CH1]

### Agreed up front (proposed)
- Publication: the company has 45 days to review any manuscript before submission; results from the controlled production trials may be published after that review period.
- Ownership: the company owns the validated operating protocol and any process adjustments derived from the trials; the Faculty of Technology retains the right to use the kinetic data and RSM model for further research and teaching.
- Data access: the company provides the two-year batch log to the Faculty under a signed confidentiality agreement; the Faculty uses it solely for this project and deletes or returns it on completion.
- Preconditions: the project starts after the data-sharing and confidentiality agreement is signed; the company confirms that at least one trained staff member is available at least one day per week for trial execution throughout the six-month period.
