## Link-Budget Recovery for LoRaWAN Meters in Reinforced-Concrete Basements

**Company:** Elekron Sistemi a.d. works in ioT and electronics hardware. [C008] Challenge C008-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Tihomir Urošević is a Research Associate in the Department of Power, Electronics and Telecommunications, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R0800]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
LoRaWAN collar devices on water and heat meters in basement meter rooms at three municipal clients receive at -120 to -125 dBm, at gateway sensitivity, losing approximately 9% of daily readings even at SF12. [C008-CH1] The company has been in SLA breach with penalty clauses threatened for six months, and fixed-price municipal contracts bar replacing gateways or re-running cables. [C008-CH1] Repositioning gateway antennas and adding a repeater node brought limited improvement; the ReachRate applied research project must deliver a solution within 14 months. [C008-CH1, C008/news/2024-09-03/Elekron Sistemi Receives Provincial Innovation Fund Grant for LoRaWAN Signal Research] A Vojvodina Development Fund grant of 4.2 million dinars covers approximately 55% of projected project costs. [C008/news/2024-09-03/Elekron Sistemi Receives Provincial Innovation Fund Grant for LoRaWAN Signal Research]

### Prior work
- A 2025 study reports that the number and material composition of obstacles, together with spreading factor and transmit power settings, measurably shift RSSI and SNR in real indoor LoRa deployments. [W4416363698]
- A 2019 study reports that LoRa energy consumption in multifloor buildings varies up to 145 times across parameter settings, and that adaptive data rate selection is important for energy-limited indoor applications. [W2979511419]

### Research questions
1. What is the per-wall and per-floor penetration loss profile in the target basement rooms at 868 MHz, and which propagation mechanism (material loss, multipath, diffraction) accounts for the observed 9% packet drop — measured by a channel-sounding campaign across the three municipal sites and compared against a calibrated path-loss model?
2. Can a compact 868 MHz antenna modification on the collar device, optimised for near-surface mounting in a metal meter enclosure, recover enough link margin to bring missed reads below 1% without changing the gateway or running new cable — measured by field packet-delivery trials at all three sites under normal operating conditions and within LoRaWAN duty-cycle and Class A/C constraints?

### Approach
- Conduct a channel-sounding campaign in the basement meter rooms using vector network analyser and software-defined radio equipment to measure path loss, delay spread, and wall-penetration loss at 868 MHz, applying the empirical and deterministic methods used in the researcher's indoor and material-penetration propagation work.
- Build a per-site link-budget model, calibrated against the measured data, to identify which loss contributions account for the margin shortfall and to bound how much gain a device-side antenna change can recover — following the link-budget prediction approach from the researcher's LoRaWAN planning work.
- Design and prototype one or two compact 868 MHz antenna variants (drawing on the researcher's planar inverted-F antenna work for LoRa) optimised for the constrained geometry of the collar device mounted on a metal meter housing in a low-ceiling reinforced concrete room.
- Run field packet-delivery trials at all three municipal sites with the prototype antenna — logging packet delivery ratio, RSSI, and SNR over at least two full billing cycles — with all LoRaWAN parameters held within duty-cycle limits and Class A/C constraints, to measure whether missed reads fall below 1%.

### First six months
- Week 1–2: In month 1, the researcher's group visits the worst-performing meter room at one municipal site with the SDR channel sounder and vector network analyser to collect baseline RSSI and path-loss measurements; the company's RF engineer attends to provide site access and device configuration.
- Month 1: The company's senior RF engineer arranges site access at the first municipal location; the master's student sets up the channel-sounding rig (vector network analyser and SDR sounder from the faculty lab) and takes baseline wall-penetration and path-loss measurements.
- Months 2–3: Measurement campaign completes across all three sites; the master's student processes data into per-site path-loss and delay-spread profiles; the researcher reviews and calibrates the model against the existing RSSI logs.
- Month 3: The researcher and company engineer hold a joint review meeting to confirm the dominant loss mechanism at each site and agree the antenna design brief (form factor, gain target, mounting constraints) for the collar prototype work in months 4–7.

### Agreed up front (proposed)

- Ownership: the company owns the calibrated site models, the antenna design files, and any trained firmware parameters; the faculty retains the measurement methods and may reuse them in future research.
- Data access: the company provides six months of existing RSSI and PDR logs at project start under a signed data-use agreement; field access to municipal sites is arranged by the company's engineer for each measurement visit.
- Preconditions: the project starts only after the data-use agreement is signed by both parties and site-access approval is confirmed in writing by all three municipal clients.
