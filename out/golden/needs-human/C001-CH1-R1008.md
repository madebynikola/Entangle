## Unsupervised Site-Adaptive Anomaly Detection for Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP On-Device Deployment

**Company:** ArmaturaSec d.o.o. works in cybersecurity. [C001] Challenge C001-CH1.  
**Researcher:** Krisztina Kiss is a Full Professor in the Department of Computing and Control Engineering, Faculty of Technical Sciences. [R1008]  
**Format:** joint project, one page.

### The problem
ArmaturaSec has terabytes of normal Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP traffic from three years of client deployments but zero confirmed attack examples, making supervised classifiers unusable. [C001-CH1] Training on public ICS datasets produced unacceptable false positive rates in real deployments because the models cannot capture site-specific polling patterns and register usage. [C001/blog/2024-05-20/Why We Stopped Trying to Use Public ICS Datasets for Anomaly Detection] The detector must run on-device on the company's existing hardware without degrading packet forwarding latency, and must be explainable to plant managers in plain language. [C001-CH1, C001]

### Prior work
- DeepAnT uses unlabelled time-series data and a CNN-based predictor to forecast the next timestamp, then an anomaly detector module tags each timestamp as normal or abnormal; it detects point anomalies, contextual anomalies, and discords. [W2906498146]

### Research questions
1. Can a per-site one-class classifier trained only on the company's own Modbus TCP traffic archives detect behaviour that deviates from that site's normal polling patterns, measured by precision and recall on a held-out set of synthetic deviations injected into real captures?
2. Does beta-mixture-model threshold calibration from unlabelled operational data reduce the false positive rate at BACnet/IP sites to an agreed acceptable level, measured against the company engineer's manual review of flagged events over a 30-day live trial?
3. Can a quantisation-and-pruning compressed model meet on-device inference latency requirements on the company's target hardware, measured by packet forwarding delay before and after model deployment?

### Approach
- Extract per-session and per-register-address feature vectors from Modbus TCP captures using the statistical baseline modelling approach from the researcher's one-class classification work on Modbus/TCP, then train a per-site one-class classifier on the company's own traffic archives — not public datasets.
- Apply beta mixture model threshold calibration to each site's unlabelled operational stream, estimating the contamination fraction without requiring labelled attack examples, so the decision boundary adapts to each deployment's traffic volume and periodicity.
- Compress the trained per-site models using quantisation and pruning to fit within the memory and compute budget of the company's on-device hardware, verifying that packet forwarding latency does not increase beyond an agreed ceiling.
- Map model output scores to a small set of named alert categories tied to observable protocol events (e.g. unexpected function code, new device address, register write outside normal range), giving plant managers plain-language flags without requiring them to interpret detector scores.

### First six months
- Month 1 — data transfer: company engineer identifies three representative client site captures (with client consent confirmed), anonymises as needed, and transfers to the researcher's lab under a signed data-sharing agreement.
- Month 1–2 — feature extraction setup: master's student integrates the company's existing C/Rust Modbus TCP and BACnet/IP parser as a library, runs it against transferred captures, and produces session-level and register-level feature vectors; company engineer reviews output for correctness.
- Month 2–3 — baseline characterisation: master's student plots traffic periodicity, function-code distributions, and register-address ranges per site to document what normal looks like at each; researcher and company engineer agree on which features are site-adaptive versus protocol-generic.
- Month 3–6 — first models: master's student trains one-class SVM and statistical baseline models per site on Modbus TCP data, applies beta mixture model calibration to set thresholds, injects synthetic deviations into held-out captures, and reports precision, false positive rate, and threshold sensitivity.

### Why this pair
ArmaturaSec has real industrial traffic at scale but cannot build an unsupervised detector without the specific methods needed to work with no attack labels and to deploy on constrained hardware. Krisztina Kiss has published on one-class classification for Modbus/TCP, label-free threshold calibration, and on-device model compression, and has collaborated with a regional energy distributor and a water utility as monitoring data partners on two funded projects. The company brings the data, the parser, the hardware, and the deployment context; the researcher brings the methods, the testbed for controlled evaluation, and a master's student to do the day-to-day modelling work.

### Risks and constraints
- The records on long-term traffic characterisation and baseline construction cover discrete manufacturing, water utility, and district heating sites (SYN-PUB-10474) and substation automation networks (SYN-PUB-10472); coverage of BACnet/IP-specific baseline behaviour at building-automation sites is not documented in those records. [SYN-PUB-10474, SYN-PUB-10472]

### Agreed up front (proposed)
- Publication: either party may submit findings for publication after a 60-day written review by the company; the company may request a delay of up to 90 additional days to protect commercially sensitive site configurations, but may not block publication indefinitely.
- Ownership: the company owns the trained per-site models, the compressed firmware artefacts, and the plain-language alert taxonomy; the University of Novi Sad retains the general methods, the evaluation framework, and the right to use anonymised, aggregated results in future research.
- Data access: the company transfers traffic captures under a signed data-sharing agreement that limits use to this project; captures remain on University infrastructure behind agreed access controls and are deleted or returned at project end; no raw captures appear in any publication.
- Preconditions: client consent for research use of their traffic must be confirmed in writing before data transfer begins; the project starts only after the data-sharing agreement is signed by both parties.
